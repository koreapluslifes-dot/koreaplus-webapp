Cost per use: the only honest way to compare two skincare products

⏱ 2 min read
⚡ Quick answer: Cost per use — purchase price divided by the number of applications you will realistically get out of the container — is the only figure that lets you compare a small ampoule against a large tub without fooling yourself. It routinely reverses first impressions: a dear-looking serum used four drops at a time can be cheaper per application than a cheap cream you apply by the fingerful. The number only holds if you finish the product, so it should always be checked against the shelf life once opened.

How to work it out in thirty seconds

Take the price, divide by the volume in millilitres, then multiply by your typical dose. A pea-sized amount of serum is roughly a fifth of a millilitre; a face-and-neck layer of sunscreen is closer to a millilitre and a half. Sheet masks skip the arithmetic entirely because they are already sold per use, which is precisely why they look cheap and behave expensively. Do this for three products you are choosing between and the ranking changes more often than not.

Where it overturns your intuition

Concentrated formats — ampoules, drops, high-active serums — tend to win, because they are used sparingly by design. Rich creams and cleansing balms tend to lose, because generous dosing is part of using them properly. Sunscreen sits awkwardly in the middle: correct application is deliberately generous, so a cheap tube used at full dose beats an expensive one rationed thin. That single comparison is the strongest practical argument for buying sunscreen at drugstore tier and buying it often.

Where the number quietly lies

Cost per use assumes you reach the bottom of the container. A large size of an unstable formula — an ascorbic acid serum, a retinoid — may oxidise or pass its period-after-opening window before you get there, at which point the true cost per use is the price divided by however many applications you managed. The open-jar symbol with a number and an M is the correction to apply. Buy the size you will finish inside that window, not the size with the flattering headline maths.

Use it as a rule, not a calculation

Nobody does this arithmetic every time, so convert it into thresholds. Decide roughly what a single application of each category is worth to you, and let anything far above it justify itself explicitly. Sheet masks become a considered treat rather than a habit. A luxury eye cream has to argue its case against the same money spent on more sunscreen. The point is not thrift for its own sake — it is that comparing sticker prices across formats is comparing nothing at all.
