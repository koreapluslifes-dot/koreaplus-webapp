Sunscreen is the highest-value purchase in any skincare budget

⏱ 2 min read

Why it wins the evidence argument

Among everything sold for the visible signs of ageing, photoprotection has the strongest and least contested support. Ultraviolet exposure is the best-documented external contributor to how lined, mottled and slack skin comes to look over decades, and daily protection is the intervention aimed directly at it. No serum in the library has a comparable weight of evidence behind it. That is why a cheap sunscreen used every morning outranks an expensive serum used alongside bare skin, and it is not a close call.

Korean sunscreen is cheap for what it is

Korea approves a broader set of modern organic UV filters than some markets, and the local formulating tradition prizes light, non-greasy, makeup-compatible finishes. The result is a category where drugstore-tier products often feel better than considerably dearer imports, carry high UVA protection grades, and cost little per tube. One regulatory caveat matters: filters permitted in Korea are not permitted everywhere, so a product bought abroad may not be sold or regulated the same way at home. Check the label of what is sold in your own country.

The value only exists at the right dose

Protection is measured at an application density most people never reach — roughly two finger-lengths for the face and neck, with reapplication across long days outdoors. Applied thinner, a high-rated sunscreen behaves like a lower-rated one. That is the practical case for buying cheaply and generously rather than dearly and carefully. If the price of a tube makes you hesitate before the second pump, the tube is too expensive for you regardless of what the box claims.

When a pricier sunscreen still earns it

Adherence is the whole game, so a formula that leaves no cast on deeper skin tones, sits under makeup without pilling, or does not sting your eyes is worth real money if it converts you from occasional to daily. The same logic covers tinted versions people wear instead of foundation. What extra money does not buy is better protection at equal rating. Pay for the thing that changes your behaviour, and stop paying the moment it does not.
