Sunscreen on retinoid-treated skin: the practical problems nobody mentions

Why it matters more here than usual

Retinoid-treated skin is routinely described as more sun-sensitive, and tretinoin itself is degraded by UV, which is part of why topical retinoids are typically applied at night. There is also a simpler argument. If you are using a retinoid for the look of tone, texture or fine lines, unprotected sun exposure is working directly against the outcome. Sunscreen is not a supporting act in this routine; it is the half of it that runs in daylight, and skipping it makes the other half largely decorative.

The flaking problem, honestly

Sunscreen applied over peeling skin goes on unevenly, catches on rough patches and pills under makeup, and no formula fully solves it. What helps is preparation rather than product-hopping: a hydrating layer underneath so the surface is not gripping, a moisturiser given a moment to settle, and application by patting rather than dragging. Some people find a mineral formula sits worse on flaking, others find the opposite. Reapplication over a patchy surface protects less well than a fresh full layer, which is worth knowing rather than pretending otherwise.

Where Korean formulation genuinely helps

Korea permits a set of modern UV filters that let formulators build light fluids and gels rather than heavy creams, and that texture advantage is not vanity when your face is already uncomfortable. The local vocabulary is useful when shopping: 무기자차 (mugijacha) for mineral filters, 유기자차 (yugijacha) for organic ones, 혼합자차 (honhapjacha) for a mix. Look for the PA rating alongside SPF for UVA coverage. Pick something you will genuinely reapply — a sunscreen abandoned by July protects nobody, regardless of the number on the tube.

Reapplication in real life

Sticks and cushions are the common Korean workaround for topping up over makeup, and they are a compromise rather than an equivalent: convenient, and usually applied more thinly than the layer they are topping up. Shade, a hat and long sleeves do work that no formula matches, and they cost nothing to reapply. If you are on a prescription retinoid, ask your clinician whether anything else in your plan raises sun sensitivity further, since some oral medications carry their own caution.
