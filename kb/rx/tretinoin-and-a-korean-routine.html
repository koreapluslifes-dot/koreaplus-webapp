Using a Korean routine while you are on tretinoin

⏱ 3 min read

Why tretinoin usually gets the night to itself

Tretinoin is a prescription retinoid rather than a cosmetic retinol, and it does not need assistance. The early phase most people describe — tightness, flaking, rough patches beside the nose and mouth — gets worse when anything else exfoliates or stings. That is why routines shrink around it instead of growing. AHAs, BHAs, low-pH vitamin C, grain scrubs and the 각질 (gakjil, 'dead skin') removing gels sold everywhere in Korea are the first things people are commonly asked to set aside. If you want to keep one, raise it at your appointment rather than deciding by feel.

What the Korean half of the routine is actually for

What survives is the hydration and 진정 (jinjeong, 'calming') side: a watery toner or essence pressed in, a panthenol- or centella-leaning cream, a fragrance-free balm for the dry corners. None of this makes a prescription work better, and the popular line that hydrating layers 'boost penetration' is not something to bank on. What they do is make skin feel liveable while it adjusts, and comfort is not a trivial goal here. The most common reason a retinoid course goes nowhere is that the person stopped using it.

The layering rules people repeat, and who should answer them

Moisturiser first as a buffer. Alternating nights. Dry skin rather than damp. Leaving a gap after cleansing. All of these circulate as fixed rules, and all of them are really just levers for how much irritation a given person gets. Some prescribers suggest them, some do not, and the evidence separating them is thinner than the confidence with which they are repeated. Bring the question to the person who knows your skin, your climate and the rest of your shelf. A comment section knows none of those things.

The part nobody gets to skip

Sunscreen, every morning, reapplied when you are out. Retinoid-treated skin is commonly described as more sun-sensitive, tretinoin itself is degraded by UV, and unprotected exposure works against whatever change in tone or texture you were hoping for. This is where Korean formulation genuinely helps: the local sunscreen category is built around light fluids and gels that sit tolerably on flaking skin, where a thick cream can feel impossible. Pick the one you will actually reapply rather than the highest number on the tube.

What is the 10-step Korean skincare routine?

The 10-step Korean skincare routine is a layered framework popularized in Korea: oil cleanser, water cleanser, exfoliant, toner, essence, serum or ampoule, sheet mask, eye cream, moisturizer and sunscreen. It's a customizable menu, not a daily mandate, and most people use only the steps their skin needs.

What is a good Korean skincare routine for beginners?

A simple beginner Korean routine is a gentle cleanser, a hydrating toner, a lightweight moisturizer and daily sunscreen, adding one targeted serum once your skin adjusts. Introduce actives slowly, one at a time, and patch-test, following the Korean principle of gentle, barrier-friendly care over many steps at once.

How many steps should a Korean skincare routine have?

As few as three: cleanser, moisturiser and sunscreen. The ten-step routine was a Western marketing frame rather than a Korean rule — most Koreans use a handful of products daily and add extras occasionally.

How often should you exfoliate in a Korean routine?

Once or twice a week for most people, up to three times for oily and resilient skin. Frequency matters far more than strength — a mild acid used consistently beats a strong one that forces you to stop.
