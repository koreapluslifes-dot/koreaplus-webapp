Cleansing around a prescription: the step people get wrong first

⏱ 3 min read
⚡ Quick answer: On any prescription active, the cleanser is usually the first thing worth changing and the last thing people think about — a high-foaming, squeaky-clean wash makes a medicated routine considerably more uncomfortable than it needs to be. The general pattern is one mild low-foam or cream cleanser in the evening, water or a very light rinse in the morning, no brushes and no scrubs. How cleansing should sit around your specific medication is worth asking your prescriber.

Squeaky is a warning, not a result

The tight, squeaking feeling after washing is widely marketed as cleanliness and is better read as over-stripping, especially on skin already being dried by a prescription. Low-foam gels, cream cleansers, milks and balms all clean adequately; the foam is largely theatre. Lukewarm water rather than hot, a short wash rather than a lengthy massage, and patting rather than rubbing dry are the unglamorous adjustments that make the biggest difference. If your cleanser is the reason your face stings when the medication goes on, change the cleanser.

Does double cleansing still apply?

It applies when there is something to remove. If you wear sunscreen, makeup or a heavy sun stick, an oil or balm first genuinely helps get it off without scrubbing, and 이중세안 (ijungsean, 'double cleansing') exists for that reason rather than as a daily ritual requirement. If you wear none of those, one gentle wash is enough and two is just more disruption. The morning is where most people over-cleanse; water alone, or a very light cleanser, is a common recommendation for medicated skin.

What to remove from the sink entirely

Cleansing brushes, konjac sponges used vigorously, scrub cleansers with grains, 각질 (gakjil) removing gels, and cleansers built around acids while you are on a prescription active. Korean cleansers frequently contain low levels of AHA or BHA without shouting about it, so the ingredient list matters more than the marketing. Hot water and long steamy showers belong on the same list. None of this is about being precious — it is about not doing the medication's job twice on skin that only has one surface.

Timing, and the question to ask

Whether to apply a medication straight after washing or to let skin dry first, and whether moisturiser goes before or after, are the two questions people improvise most often. Both change how much irritation a given person gets, and both have real answers for your specific prescription — from the person who wrote it. Ask at the appointment, and ask again if the routine changes. In the meantime, keep the cleanser boring, because it is the one step you repeat every single day without thinking.

What is the 10-step Korean skincare routine?

The 10-step Korean skincare routine is a layered framework popularized in Korea: oil cleanser, water cleanser, exfoliant, toner, essence, serum or ampoule, sheet mask, eye cream, moisturizer and sunscreen. It's a customizable menu, not a daily mandate, and most people use only the steps their skin needs.

What is double cleansing?

Double cleansing is a two-step Korean method: first an oil-based cleanser dissolves makeup, sunscreen and sebum, then a water-based cleanser removes sweat and water-soluble debris. The pairing aims to clean thoroughly without the tightness or stripping that one harsh wash can cause.

What is a first essence and do I need one?

A first essence is a watery, high-percentage ferment applied straight after cleansing, before anything else. It is a hydration and conditioning step. Pleasant and popular, but genuinely optional.
