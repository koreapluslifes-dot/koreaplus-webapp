Adapalene alongside Korean skincare: what usually changes

How it behaves differently

Adapalene binds more selectively than older retinoids and is reported to be better tolerated at comparable use, though 'better tolerated' still means many people get a dry, tight, peeling stretch. Two practical differences matter. It is more stable in light, and it is stable alongside benzoyl peroxide, where tretinoin is classically broken down by it — which is why fixed combination products pair adapalene with peroxide rather than tretinoin. In Korea it generally comes through a clinic or pharmacy rather than off a cosmetics shelf, so it is not something to add casually on a trip.

What comes out of the routine

The same list as any prescription retinoid: acid toners, peeling pads, enzyme powders, scrub cleansers, cleansing brushes and the strong low-pH vitamin C serums that already sting on a good day. Korean routines make this harder than it sounds, because exfoliation is often built into products that do not look like exfoliants — 필링 (piling) pads sold as a toner step, gommage sticks, 'texture refining' essences. Read the ingredient list rather than the marketing line, and if you are unsure, put it aside until you can ask.

What is worth keeping

A gentle cleanser that does not leave skin squeaking, a simple hydrating layer, a moisturiser weighted towards ceramides, panthenol or centella, and sunscreen. Korea's strength here is texture: light gel creams for oily skin that still want comfort, and thicker 크림 (keurim) or balm textures for the flaking phase. Fragrance is the thing to watch — a great many otherwise sensible Korean moisturisers are scented, and that is exactly what most clinicians would rather you avoid while skin is reacting.

Purging, patience and honest expectations

An early flare gets called purging and the term does real work for some people and cover for others. A retinoid speeding up what was already forming under the surface is plausible; a rash, swelling, weeping or spreading irritation is not purging and is worth reporting. Nobody can tell you from a distance which one you have. What is reasonably clear is that retinoids reward consistency over intensity, and that stopping and restarting repeatedly produces the irritation without the payoff. Your prescriber decides what to do if the reaction is not settling.

What is the 10-step Korean skincare routine?

The 10-step Korean skincare routine is a layered framework popularized in Korea: oil cleanser, water cleanser, exfoliant, toner, essence, serum or ampoule, sheet mask, eye cream, moisturizer and sunscreen. It's a customizable menu, not a daily mandate, and most people use only the steps their skin needs.

What is a good Korean skincare routine for beginners?

A simple beginner Korean routine is a gentle cleanser, a hydrating toner, a lightweight moisturizer and daily sunscreen, adding one targeted serum once your skin adjusts. Introduce actives slowly, one at a time, and patch-test, following the Korean principle of gentle, barrier-friendly care over many steps at once.

How many steps should a Korean skincare routine have?

As few as three: cleanser, moisturiser and sunscreen. The ten-step routine was a Western marketing frame rather than a Korean rule — most Koreans use a handful of products daily and add extras occasionally.

How often should you exfoliate in a Korean routine?

Once or twice a week for most people, up to three times for oily and resilient skin. Frequency matters far more than strength — a mild acid used consistently beats a strong one that forces you to stop.
