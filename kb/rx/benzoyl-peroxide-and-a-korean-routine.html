Benzoyl peroxide beside Korean skincare: what it wrecks, what it needs

The laundry problem is real, and it is permanent

Benzoyl peroxide bleaches cotton. Towels, pillowcases, flannel headbands, the collar of a favourite shirt, and hair if a leave-on product migrates overnight. There is no fixing the marks afterwards. White bedding and white towels are the standard workaround, along with letting it dry properly before anything touches your face. This sounds trivial next to the skincare questions and it is the single thing people are most annoyed about missing. It also applies to a partner's pillowcase, which is a conversation worth having in advance.

What it does and does not combine with

Benzoyl peroxide is an oxidiser, and it is classically described as degrading tretinoin when the two are applied together, while adapalene is stable alongside it — which is why fixed combinations pair peroxide with adapalene. The same oxidising nature is why people are often told to keep it away from a pure vitamin C serum in the same layer. Clinicians commonly prescribe it with a topical antibiotic specifically to reduce the chance of bacterial resistance developing. How any of these are sequenced across a day is a prescription question, not a preference.

The storage point worth knowing

There has been an ongoing regulatory conversation about benzene forming in benzoyl peroxide products, particularly when they are stored warm, and it has produced recalls in some markets. The sensible domestic response is unglamorous: keep the tube cool, do not leave it in a car in a Korean August, do not stockpile it on a sunny windowsill, and check expiry. Where that conversation currently stands is something a pharmacist can tell you more reliably than a search result, and it is a fair question to ask when you collect it.

The Korean products that pair sensibly

Comfort products, essentially. A cream cleanser or low-foam gel rather than a squeaky high-pH foam, a plain hydrating toner, and a moisturiser leaning on ceramides, 판테놀 (panteneol, panthenol) or centella. Korea's 진정 (jinjeong, 'calming') category is genuinely useful alongside a drying prescription — provided you pick the fragrance-free, alcohol-light versions rather than the ones that tingle. What does not pair sensibly is another acne active bought on top. Two drying products is not twice the result, it is twice the irritation, and it is how people end up stopping.

What is the 10-step Korean skincare routine?

The 10-step Korean skincare routine is a layered framework popularized in Korea: oil cleanser, water cleanser, exfoliant, toner, essence, serum or ampoule, sheet mask, eye cream, moisturizer and sunscreen. It's a customizable menu, not a daily mandate, and most people use only the steps their skin needs.

What is a good Korean skincare routine for beginners?

A simple beginner Korean routine is a gentle cleanser, a hydrating toner, a lightweight moisturizer and daily sunscreen, adding one targeted serum once your skin adjusts. Introduce actives slowly, one at a time, and patch-test, following the Korean principle of gentle, barrier-friendly care over many steps at once.

How many steps should a Korean skincare routine have?

As few as three: cleanser, moisturiser and sunscreen. The ten-step routine was a Western marketing frame rather than a Korean rule — most Koreans use a handful of products daily and add extras occasionally.

How often should you exfoliate in a Korean routine?

Once or twice a week for most people, up to three times for oily and resilient skin. Frequency matters far more than strength — a mild acid used consistently beats a strong one that forces you to stop.
