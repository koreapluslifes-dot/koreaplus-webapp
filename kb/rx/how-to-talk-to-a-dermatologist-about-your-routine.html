Bringing your existing Korean routine to a dermatology appointment

Photographs of ingredient lists work better than brand names, especially for imported Korean products a clinician outside Korea will not recognise. Every recommendation that follows is theirs to make; your job is to make sure they are working from real information.

Photograph the ingredient lists, not the bottles

A brand name tells a clinician nothing if the product is Korean and they are not. The ingredient list tells them everything, and Korean packaging carries a full 전성분 (jeonseongbun, 'full ingredient list') by law. Photograph the back of each product before the appointment. Where the list is printed in Korean only, most items are still recognisable INCI names transliterated into Hangul, and a translation app plus the pictures gets you most of the way. Include cleansers, sunscreen and anything from a bathhouse or hotel.

Declare the things that do not feel like skincare

Supplements, oral medications from other doctors, recent salon treatments, brow threading, waxing, home devices, LED masks, dermarollers, and anything a friend gave you from an unlabelled tube. Also declare what you are planning: a peel booked next month, a Korea trip built around clinic appointments, a wedding. Clinicians make different decisions when they know a laser is coming. Omitting things because they feel embarrassing or trivial is the main way a consultation goes wrong, and nobody in that room is judging your shelf.

Ask better questions than 'is this good?'

Useful ones: which of these should stop, and for how long. Which can stay. What should I expect in the first stretch, and what would count as a reaction rather than an adjustment. What do I do if that happens, and how do I reach you. When do things come back, and in what order. Is there anything here that makes me more sun-sensitive. If cost or availability is a constraint, say so — substitutions are usually possible and are better decided in the room.

If you are consulting in Korea as a visitor

Bring your product photographs and a written list of medications with generic names rather than brand names, since brands differ by country. Ask what the follow-up arrangement is if something goes wrong after you fly home, because the honest answer is often that a doctor where you live becomes responsible. Ask for the aftercare instructions in writing. Interpretation is available at many 피부과 (pibugwa, dermatology clinics) in Seoul, and it is worth requesting rather than improvising through a phone.

What is the 10-step Korean skincare routine?

The 10-step Korean skincare routine is a layered framework popularized in Korea: oil cleanser, water cleanser, exfoliant, toner, essence, serum or ampoule, sheet mask, eye cream, moisturizer and sunscreen. It's a customizable menu, not a daily mandate, and most people use only the steps their skin needs.

What is a good Korean skincare routine for beginners?

A simple beginner Korean routine is a gentle cleanser, a hydrating toner, a lightweight moisturizer and daily sunscreen, adding one targeted serum once your skin adjusts. Introduce actives slowly, one at a time, and patch-test, following the Korean principle of gentle, barrier-friendly care over many steps at once.

How many steps should a Korean skincare routine have?

As few as three: cleanser, moisturiser and sunscreen. The ten-step routine was a Western marketing frame rather than a Korean rule — most Koreans use a handful of products daily and add extras occasionally.

How often should you exfoliate in a Korean routine?

Once or twice a week for most people, up to three times for oily and resilient skin. Frequency matters far more than strength — a mild acid used consistently beats a strong one that forces you to stop.
