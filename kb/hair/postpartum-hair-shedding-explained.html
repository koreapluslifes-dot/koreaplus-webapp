Postpartum shedding: common, temporary, and not a product failure

⏱ 2 min read
⚡ Quick answer: Shedding a few months after childbirth is common and is generally attributed to hair that stayed in its growing phase during pregnancy moving into shedding all at once. It usually settles on its own over the following months. No shampoo prevents it, and if shedding is heavy, prolonged, patchy, or accompanied by other symptoms, that is a conversation for a doctor rather than a shop assistant.

Shedding a few months after childbirth is common and is generally attributed to hair that stayed in its growing phase during pregnancy moving into shedding all at once. It usually settles on its own over the following months. No shampoo prevents it, and if shedding is heavy, prolonged, patchy, or accompanied by other symptoms, that is a conversation for a doctor rather than a shop assistant.

What is happening

During pregnancy a larger share of hair tends to stay in its growing phase instead of shedding on the usual rolling schedule. After birth that synchronisation unwinds and the delayed shedding arrives together, typically a couple of months later, which is why it looks so alarming in the shower. It is less that more hair is being lost overall and more that months of ordinary shedding have been compressed into weeks. It commonly settles over the following months, though the timeline varies considerably.

The Korean context

Korea has a well-developed postpartum care culture, 산후조리 (sanhujori), including dedicated postpartum centres, and a body of traditional advice about staying warm and avoiding cold that historically extended to not washing the hair for weeks. Modern practice is far more relaxed, and there is no reason to leave the scalp uncomfortable; a clean scalp is easier to live with than a neglected one. The genuinely useful part of the tradition is its underlying principle — rest, food and help — which matters more here than any product.

What helps in the meantime

Handle the hair gently: a wide-tooth comb, loose ties rather than tight ponytails, minimal heat and no chemical services while you are already anxious about the mirror. A shorter or layered cut makes shedding less visible and is easier to manage with a baby. Regrowth at the hairline often returns as short wispy pieces that refuse to lie flat for a while — that is the recovery showing, not a new problem. Resist panic-buying an anti-hair-loss line, because this is not what those are for.

When to ask a doctor

See a clinician if shedding is still heavy well beyond the first year, if it is patchy rather than diffuse, if the scalp itself is sore or scaly, or if it comes with fatigue, weight change or feeling cold — iron levels and thyroid function are worth checking and neither is something to guess at. Breastfeeding also affects what any prescribed option can be. This page is general information; the specifics of your own recovery belong in an appointment.
