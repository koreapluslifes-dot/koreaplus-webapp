Dandruff or dry scalp? The distinction that changes what you buy

Dandruff — 비듬 (bideum) — usually comes with an oily scalp, larger yellowish flakes and persistent itch, and is associated with a yeast that lives on everyone's skin. A dry scalp sheds small, fine, white flakes and normally comes with dry skin elsewhere. They call for opposite products, which is why the wrong shampoo so often makes things worse. Persistent redness, thick scale or soreness deserves a doctor.

Two different problems

Dandruff is generally associated with Malassezia, a yeast present on everyone's scalp, and with the inflammatory response some people mount to it in the presence of sebum. Its flakes tend to be larger, oilier and yellowish, with itch and often a background of redness. A dry scalp is dehydration and barrier disruption: small, fine, white flakes, a tight feeling, and usually dry skin elsewhere on the body. The scalp is oily in one case and not in the other, which is the fastest thing to check.

Opposite shelves

Dandruff responds to antifungal and anti-flaking actives — zinc pyrithione, piroctone olamine, ketoconazole, selenium sulfide, with salicylic acid to loosen scale — used consistently and left on for a minute or two before rinsing. A dry scalp wants the reverse: less frequent, gentler washing, a low-sulfate cleanser and leave-on hydration with panthenol, glycerin or ceramides. Using a medicated anti-dandruff shampoo on a scalp that is merely dry reliably makes it worse, and is the most common self-inflicted scalp problem there is.

How Korean packaging sorts it

Anti-dandruff actives in Korea often place a shampoo in the 의약외품 (uiyagoepum, quasi-drug) category, which is printed on the bottle and is a useful signal that a real active is present at a working level. Products aimed at a dry, tight scalp are instead labelled with 진정 (jinjeong, calming) and 보습 (boseup, moisturising) and sit in the ordinary cosmetics category. Reading those few words saves a great deal of guesswork in an aisle where every bottle looks much like the next.

When it is neither

Persistent redness, thick adherent scale, weeping, soreness, hair loss in the affected patches, or flaking that ignores months of the correct products is not a shopping problem. Seborrhoeic dermatitis, scalp psoriasis and contact reactions to hair dye all present as 'dandruff that will not go away' and are managed differently by a clinician. A dermatologist can distinguish them in a short appointment, and in Korea that appointment is easy to get. Do not spend a year cycling through shampoos to avoid it.

What is the difference between essence and serum?

In Korean skincare, essence is typically a thinner, hydration-focused layer that preps skin, while serum is more concentrated with targeted actives like vitamin C or niacinamide. Essence usually comes first; serum follows. The line between them blurs, and not every routine needs both.

What is the difference between AHA and BHA?

AHAs like glycolic and lactic acid are water-soluble and work on the skin's surface to brighten and smooth, while BHA like salicylic acid is oil-soluble and gets into pores. In Korean skincare both are used sparingly, a few times weekly, always with daily sun protection.

What is the difference between essence, serum and ampoule?

Essence is the lightest and most hydration-focused, serum is more concentrated and targeted, and ampoule is the most concentrated of the three. The boundaries are marketing convention, not regulated definitions.
