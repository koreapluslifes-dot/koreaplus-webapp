Hair loss — 탈모 (talmo) — is discussed openly in Korea and has its own aisle, its own regulated cosmetic category and a large clinic industry behind it. Korean law permits certain cosmetics to claim they help relieve the symptoms of hair loss, which is a cosmetic claim rather than a medical one. Anything that genuinely acts on hair growth is a medicine, and that conversation belongs with a doctor.

Why it is discussed openly

Hair loss carries less stigma as a topic in Korea than in many places. Dermatology clinics are numerous and relatively accessible, scalp-care aisles are large and unembarrassed, and 탈모 turns up in ordinary conversation and advertising rather than being whispered about. Younger customers, women included, buy scalp care preventively rather than after a crisis. Whether that reflects a genuinely different pattern of hair loss or simply a culture that watches appearance closely and acts early is not something anyone should claim to know precisely.

The regulated cosmetic category

Korea has a legal category called 기능성화장품 (functional cosmetics), and one of its permitted purposes is helping to relieve the symptoms of hair loss. Products in it are built around a short notified ingredient list — including things such as dexpanthenol, biotin and l-menthol — and must carry the approved wording rather than freestyle claims. That is genuinely useful for shoppers, because the sentence on the bottle has a defined meaning. It remains a cosmetic claim about symptoms and appearance, sitting well below what a prescribed medicine does.

What a shampoo can and cannot do

A good scalp shampoo removes sebum and residue, calms itching, and reduces the flaking that makes a scalp look and feel inflamed. If hair has been breaking near the root or shedding because the scalp was in poor condition, improving that condition can reduce what you see in the drain. What it does not do is alter the pattern of androgenetic hair loss, which is driven by hormones and inheritance and does not respond to a cleanser, however expensive the bottle or evocative the herb list.

Where to take it seriously

Sudden shedding, patchy bald spots, a sore or scaly scalp, hair loss alongside fatigue or weight change, or anything progressing quickly should go to a dermatologist rather than into a shopping basket. Effective options exist, they are medicines, and choosing between them depends on your pattern, sex, age and health — which is exactly why this page will not recommend one. Korean clinics handle this routinely and a consultation is inexpensive by international standards. Cosmetic scalp care sits alongside that, not instead of it.
