Choosing a Clinic as a Visitor: Licensing, Consent and Aftercare You Can Actually Reach

⏱ 2 min read
⚡ Quick answer: The checks that matter most are who will physically perform the procedure, exactly which licensed product or device is being used, what the consent document you sign actually says, and who you can contact once you have flown home. Medical procedures in Korea are restricted to licensed doctors, and institutions marketing to overseas patients are generally required to register in order to do so. This is general guidance, not a recommendation of any clinic, and every clinical decision belongs with a licensed professional in person.

Who is legally permitted to do what

Medical procedures in Korea must be carried out by a licensed physician, 의사 (uisa). A further distinction worth knowing is between a doctor holding general licensure and a 전문의 (jeonmun-ui), a specialist who has completed training in a particular field such as dermatology or plastic surgery. Both are doctors; they are not equivalent in training. Asking who will actually hold the device — not who ran the consultation — is a fair and specific question, and unlicensed staff performing medical procedures is unlawful, not merely poor practice.

Brokers, commissions and pressure

Unregistered intermediaries who steer foreign patients to clinics for a cut are a recognised problem, and Korean law restricts this kind of brokering. In practice it surfaces as touts near clinic districts, messaging-app accounts offering 'special foreigner prices', or a helpful acquaintance who happens to have a contact. The reliable signal is pressure: legitimate care rarely requires you to decide before you have read anything. If a discount depends on booking immediately or on not asking about the product being used, walk.

Consent you can actually read

Ask for consent documentation and aftercare instructions in a language you read, and take time over them rather than signing at reception with a pen already held out. Ask to see sealed product packaging before it is opened. Ask what the complication plan is and who manages it. Where interpretation is provided, note whether the interpreter is the same person selling you the package, because that is a conflict, however friendly. Photographs of documents you have signed are worth keeping for your own records.

Aftercare is the part visitors forget

Booking a procedure late in a trip leaves no margin if something needs looking at, and flights, sun and unfamiliar climate all land on skin that has just been through a procedure. Before agreeing to anything, establish who you contact in the following days, whether that contact works from another country, and what your own doctor at home would need to know. Carry the product or device details with you. This page names no clinic, recommends nothing, and states no criteria for suitability; that assessment belongs to a licensed doctor examining you in person.
