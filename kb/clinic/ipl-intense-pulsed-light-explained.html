IPL (Intense Pulsed Light): What It Is, and Why It Is Not a Laser

⏱ 2 min read
⚡ Quick answer: IPL emits a broad band of filtered light rather than the single coherent wavelength a laser produces, so calling it a laser is inaccurate even though clinics and advertising routinely blur the two. Filters narrow the output so that certain targets within skin absorb proportionally more energy than the surrounding tissue. Filter choice, settings and operator judgement matter enormously, and any decision about IPL belongs with a licensed clinician who has seen your skin in person.

The physics difference in plain terms

A laser produces light of essentially one wavelength, travelling in step. IPL produces a wide spectrum from a flashlamp, then cuts away the parts the operator does not want using filters. The practical consequence is that IPL is less selective by nature and depends more heavily on how it is configured for the person in the chair. Neither approach is inherently superior; they are different tools. What matters is that the marketing habit of calling everything a 'laser facial' erases a distinction that changes how a session should be planned.

How it shows up on Korean clinic menus

IPL is frequently folded into packaged 'photo' programmes sold as courses, sometimes bundled with other steps into a single named routine, which makes it hard to tell what you are actually buying. It is often described as the gentle option. Gentleness, though, is a function of settings rather than of the technology, and the same handpiece can be run conservatively or hard. Ask which filter and what settings are planned, and treat vague answers as a signal about how carefully the session will be run.

Where the risk genuinely sits

Burns and blistering are the classic hazards, and they are strongly linked to settings applied to skin that is tanned, naturally deeper in tone, or recently sun-exposed. Pigment can also shift in the other direction, leaving lighter patches. Eye protection is non-negotiable for both client and operator. Consumer home devices run at lower output but rely on the same principle and the same cautions, and they are frequently used on areas and skin tones their instructions exclude. Read the manufacturer's own restrictions rather than a review video.

Why this decision cannot be made from a page

Suitability for light-based work depends on individual pigmentation, recent sun exposure, medications, skin conditions and history — a combination no article can assess. This overview describes the category and its known hazards, makes no claim about outcomes, and is not a recommendation. If IPL is being suggested to you in Korea or anywhere else, have that conversation with a licensed dermatologist or physician who examines your skin directly, and ask them specifically about risk in your own skin tone rather than in general.
