Myeongdong then and now: what happened to Seoul's beauty district

⏱ 2 min read
⚡ Quick answer: Myeongdong (명동) was for years the default beauty district for visitors — a dense grid of single-brand roadshops with staff pressing free sheet masks on anyone walking past. That format thinned out as multi-brand chains and online shopping took over and tourist footfall collapsed during the pandemic. The district has refilled with a different mix of chains, tourist retail and evening street food: still worth a walk, no longer where the best prices are.

What made it the beauty district

Central location, direct transport, hotels and duty-free within walking distance, and a critical mass of shopfronts inside a few blocks. Because brands were competing for the same pavement, promotions escalated against each other: free masks, one-plus-one baskets, staff calling out in several languages, discounts that existed mainly to beat the shop next door. For roughly a decade it was the most efficient place anywhere to see the entire Korean cosmetics market in a single hour on foot.

What changed

The single-brand model lost to multi-brand shelves and to online pricing. The late-2010s fall in Chinese group tourism removed a large slice of the demand the district was built around, and the pandemic emptied units outright, leaving long stretches of shuttered frontage. Rents in a prime district do not fall as fast as footfall, so the churn was severe. What came back skewed towards operators able to serve mixed international traffic rather than one brand's estate of stores.

What it is like now

A mixture: multi-brand beauty chains, some remaining brand stores, souvenir and clothing retail, cosmetics shops aimed squarely at visitors, and one of Seoul's busiest street-food cart scenes once the evening starts. Signage in several languages, staff used to foreign customers, and a great deal of noise. It is unmistakably built for tourists now, which is a description rather than a complaint — it is a good place to eat, browse and get a sense of scale.

Shopping it sensibly

The free-mask barkers are a sales funnel, not generosity, and the bundle deals assembled for visitors are not automatically good value. Almost everything on offer sits on a chain shelf elsewhere at a chain price, so check one item you already know before committing to a haul. Carry your passport for tax refunds. The sound approach is to treat Myeongdong as somewhere to browse and eat, and to buy where prices are fixed, labelled and comparable.
