Department store counters versus roadshops: which one you actually need

⏱ 2 min read
⚡ Quick answer: Department-store beauty halls sell service and prestige Korean lines — seated consultation, shade matching under decent light, samples and gift packaging — at prices that almost never move. Chains and surviving roadshops sell volume and speed: self-service, stacked promotions, low prices and no expectation that you speak to anyone. Use the counter when the purchase genuinely needs a person, and the chain when it does not.

What a counter actually gives you

The ground floor of a 백화점 (baekhwajeom) carries Korea's prestige houses — Sulwhasoo, The History of Whoo, Hera, O Hui, Su:m37, AMOREPACIFIC — alongside imported brands. What you are buying beyond the product is time: a seat, a consultation, skin measurement of varying rigour, shade matching in light that is at least consistent, generous samples, and boxed presentation that matters a great deal if the purchase is a gift. For foundation and cushion shades this is the only setting that reliably beats guessing.

What the chains give you

Multi-brand chains and the remaining single-brand roadshops run on self-service. Nobody approaches you, nobody upsells, and equally nobody helps unless you ask. Promotions run more or less continuously and stack on top of each other. The range skews mass and mid-tier, weighted towards skincare basics and whatever is currently trending. For anything you have bought before and already know works on your skin, this is straightforwardly the better option and the cheaper one.

Price behaves differently in each

Department-store shelf prices are stable on purpose — discounting a prestige line on the shop floor undermines its positioning, so the levers used instead are sample sets, gift-with-purchase, membership tiers and seasonal boxed sets. That means the same prestige brand is genuinely cheaper in duty-free and travel retail than at its own counter. Mass-market brands work the opposite way: shelf prices move constantly, and the cheapest moment to buy them is during a chain sale week with coupons applied.

A practical split

Counter: foundation, cushions, anything needing a shade match, and gifts where the box matters. Chain: sunscreen, cleansers, masks, toners and repeat purchases. A reasonable hybrid is to get matched and sampled at a counter, then buy the full size in duty-free when you fly out. Korean counter staff respond well to a clear opening line — say what you are looking for and whether you want to be left alone, and you will generally get exactly that.

A first essence is a watery, high-percentage ferment applied straight after cleansing, before anything else. It is a hydration and conditioning step. Pleasant and popular, but genuinely optional.

Do you really need all 10 steps of Korean skincare?

No. The 10-step routine is a menu, not a requirement. Many Koreans use a simple core of cleanser, hydrating toner, moisturizer and sunscreen, adding serums, essences or masks only as their skin needs. The point is consistency and customization, not maximum steps.

Do I really need to double cleanse?

At night, if you wear sunscreen or makeup — yes. Water-based cleansers cannot break down water-resistant sunscreen, so a single cleanse leaves a film behind. In the morning, one gentle cleanse or just water is plenty.
