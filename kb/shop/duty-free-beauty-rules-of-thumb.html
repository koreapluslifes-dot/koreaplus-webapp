Duty-free beauty buying in Korea: the rules of thumb that hold

⏱ 2 min read
⚡ Quick answer: Korea runs two different systems that both get called duty-free: pre-tax-exempt shops (사전면세), where you buy without tax and collect at the airport, and post-purchase tax refunds (사후면세) on ordinary shop purchases. Duty-free reliably wins on prestige Korean lines, imported fragrance and anything that never discounts on the high street. It rarely wins on mass-market skincare, which chains already discount harder than an airport ever will.

It rarely wins on mass-market skincare, which chains already discount harder than an airport ever will.

The two systems, plainly

Downtown and airport duty-free shops sell tax-exempt stock to departing travellers. You show a passport and flight details, and goods bought downtown are not carried out of the shop — they are collected at an airport pickup counter before your flight. Ordinary shops work the other way: you pay the full price including tax and reclaim it, either deducted immediately at the till in participating stores or through a kiosk or refund counter at the airport. Different paperwork, different timing, frequently confused.

Where the saving is real

Prestige Korean skincare, imported fragrance and colour cosmetics from houses that hold their shelf prices. These lines are structurally undiscounted at department-store counters, so removing tax is the only meaningful reduction available on them anywhere in the country. Travel-retail exclusive sizes and gift sets can also work out better per millilitre than the standard line. Read the set contents before assuming equivalence, since travel-retail sets are not always composed of the same sizes or the same items as the counter range.

Where it is not

Sheet masks, mass-market sunscreens, cleansers, toners and anything routinely sold on multi-buy in a chain. Airport pricing on those categories is usually worse, and airport shopping happens under time pressure, which is reliably where people overspend. The unexciting correct answer is to buy your everyday basics in the city during a sale week and reserve duty-free for the prestige items and fragrance you could not have discounted any other way.

Practical cautions

Check your destination country's personal import allowance before loading up, since cosmetics count towards it. Check liquid and sealed-bag rules if you are connecting through another airport — a sealed duty-free bag can still be challenged at a transfer security point depending on the route. Leave real time for a downtown pickup counter before departure. Minimum spends, refund thresholds and forms are revised periodically, so confirm the current rules at the counter rather than assuming last year's still apply.
