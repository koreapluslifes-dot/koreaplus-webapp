What is genuinely cheaper in Korea, and what only feels cheaper

⏱ 2 min read
⚡ Quick answer: Genuinely cheaper in Korea: mass-market Korean skincare — sunscreens, cleansers, sheet masks, toners and mid-tier serums — especially on multi-buy in a chain, plus basics from budget variety stores and launches that reach other countries only through resellers. Not cheaper: imported Western brands, prestige Korean lines at department-store counters, and anything bought at an airport under time pressure. The genuinely cheaper categories

Mass-market Korean skincare carries an importer and distributor markup abroad that simply is not there at source, and Korean chains discount it aggressively on top. Sheet masks in bulk, gel and oil cleansers, sunscreens and mid-tier serums are the clearest wins. Budget variety stores sell competent basics — cotton pads, tools, simple moisturisers, storage — at prices that are difficult to match anywhere else. New launches also appear months earlier here and without the premium a reseller adds for getting them out of the country.

The categories that are not

Imported Western skincare and fragrance are frequently more expensive in Korea than at home, sometimes conspicuously so. Prestige Korean lines at a department-store counter are not discounted at all, though the same brand in duty-free is a different calculation entirely. Airport shops are priced for people who have run out of alternatives. And anything heavy or liquid carries a second, invisible price in baggage allowance, which is a real cost people consistently forget to count.

The false economies

Buying a year of sheet masks you will not finish before their use-by date. Stockpiling vitamin C serums and similar actives that degrade once opened. Adding items to reach a multi-buy threshold and abandoning half of them. Worth stating plainly: most staple K-beauty now ships internationally at reasonable prices, so the remaining advantage is narrower than haul culture suggests — it sits in volume basics, budget-store basics and early access to new launches, not in everything on the shelf.

Doing the maths honestly

Compare against what you would actually pay at home for the thing you would actually buy, not against the shelf price of a prestige line you were never going to purchase. Factor in the tax refund if you qualify for it, the exchange rate on the day rather than the one you remember, and any foreign transaction fee your card charges. Buy single units of anything new to your skin, and buy volume only on products you have already used long enough to know they suit you.
