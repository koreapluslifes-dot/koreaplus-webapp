The 10-Step Korean Skincare Routine

The idea of a multi-step Korean skincare routine, framed memorably as '10 steps,' became a key vehicle for K-beauty's global breakout.

Naming a routine

The concept of the '10-step Korean skincare routine' was popularized in the early-to-mid 2010s, notably by Charlotte Cho, co-founder of the U.S. retailer Soko Glam. Layering steps such as cleansing (often double cleansing), toner, essence, serum, sheet mask, moisturizer and sunscreen, the framework gave Western audiences an accessible way to understand K-beauty's methodical approach.

Education as marketing

Rather than selling a single product, the 10-step idea sold a philosophy of consistent, layered, gentle skincare. Cho's writing, including a popular skincare book, and Soko Glam's curation helped translate Korean routines for English-speaking consumers. This education-first approach mirrored Korea's longstanding skincare-counseling culture.

A flexible framework

The '10 steps' was always meant as a guideline rather than a rigid prescription, and many practitioners use fewer steps. The well-documented historical point is that this catchy framing, popularized in the early 2010s, was instrumental in making K-beauty understandable and aspirational to a global audience and helped drive its U.S. retail expansion.

Key facts
  • The 10-step routine was popularized in the early-to-mid 2010s
  • Charlotte Cho of Soko Glam is closely associated with it
  • It framed K-beauty as layered, gentle, consistent skincare
  • The 10 steps are a flexible guideline, not a strict rule
