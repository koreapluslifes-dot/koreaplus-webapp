The Amore Door-to-Door Saleswomen

⚡ Quick answer: In the 1960s, Amorepacific built a network of door-to-door 'Amore' saleswomen who brought skincare and counseling directly to Korean homes.

A defining chapter in Korean beauty history is the network of door-to-door 'Amore' saleswomen who sold and taught skincare across the country.

A new way to sell beauty

In the 1960s, Amorepacific developed a door-to-door sales system under the Amore brand. Large numbers of women, often described in distinctive uniforms, visited homes to sell cosmetics and provide skincare and makeup guidance. This direct-sales model brought beauty products and education to households across Korea at a time when retail access was limited.

Counseling and education

A notable feature was the emphasis on beauty counseling: representatives were trained in skincare and application and shared product information directly with customers. The company is also associated with early in-house publications used to train salespeople. This counseling culture reinforced the skincare-first, education-led approach that K-beauty is known for.

Social and economic role

The Amore saleswomen are often remembered as more than a sales channel: the work offered income and independence to many Korean women during a period of rapid economic change. While exact figures and individual stories vary by source, the broad, well-documented point is that door-to-door selling was central to Amorepacific's mid-century growth and to spreading skincare habits nationwide.

Key facts
  • Amorepacific built a door-to-door sales system in the 1960s
  • Saleswomen sold cosmetics and gave skincare counseling at home
  • The model spread skincare education across Korea
  • The work provided income and independence to many women
