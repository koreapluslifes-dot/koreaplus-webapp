PDRN: The 'Salmon DNA' Skincare Trend Explained

PDRN — polydeoxyribonucleotide, popularly marketed as "salmon DNA" — became one of the buzziest K-beauty ingredient trends of the 2020s. Its story spans medical and aesthetic-clinic use before crossing into mainstream topical skincare marketing.

Medical and aesthetic origins

PDRN is a DNA-fragment-derived compound, often sourced from salmon, that has been studied and used in medical and aesthetic contexts and is associated in clinics with skin-renewal and "salmon" injectable treatments. This clinical heritage gave the ingredient a science-forward, professional aura when it migrated into consumer products.

Crossover into topical skincare

Korean brands brought PDRN into topical serums, ampoules, and creams, marketing it around skin-renewal, repair, and rejuvenation themes. The "salmon DNA" nickname — vivid and memorable — helped the ingredient stand out and become a recognizable trend keyword in beauty content.

Virality and caveats

PDRN gained strong momentum through social-video skincare culture in the 2020s. As with many trend ingredients, consumers should note that the effects of in-clinic injectable treatments and over-the-counter topical formulas are not equivalent, and topical claims should be read carefully against the evidence for any specific product.

Key facts
  • PDRN stands for polydeoxyribonucleotide, popularly called "salmon DNA."
  • It is often sourced from salmon-derived DNA fragments.
  • PDRN has medical and aesthetic-clinic uses preceding topical skincare.
  • Topical formulas are not equivalent to in-clinic injectable treatments.
