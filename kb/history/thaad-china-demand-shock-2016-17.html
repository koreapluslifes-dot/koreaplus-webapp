THAAD and the China Demand Shock

K-beauty's heavy reliance on Chinese demand became a vulnerability during the 2016-17 THAAD tensions, reshaping the industry's strategy.

A geopolitical jolt

Around 2016-2017, tensions arose after South Korea agreed to deploy the U.S. THAAD missile-defense system, which strained relations with China. Reported informal restrictions and a downturn in Chinese tourism and consumer demand significantly affected Korean industries that depended heavily on Chinese buyers, including cosmetics and duty-free retail.

Impact on K-beauty

Chinese consumers and tour-group shoppers had become a major source of K-beauty sales, both in China and at Korean duty-free stores. The demand shock pressured major Korean cosmetics companies that were highly exposed to that market, highlighting the risk of over-dependence on a single country for growth.

The pivot that followed

The episode pushed Korean beauty companies to diversify, accelerating efforts to grow in markets such as the United States, Southeast Asia and beyond, and to lean into digital and global e-commerce channels. Specific financial figures vary by source and are best avoided, but the structural lesson, diversify beyond China, durably shaped K-beauty's later strategy.

Key facts
  • THAAD deployment strained South Korea-China relations around 2016-17
  • Chinese demand for K-beauty and duty-free shopping fell
  • K-beauty companies heavily exposed to China were pressured
  • The shock accelerated diversification into the US and other markets
