Hormonal acne: a useful shorthand and an imprecise label

⏱ 2 min read
⚡ Quick answer: 'Hormonal acne' is not a formal diagnosis — it is shorthand for acne that tracks the menstrual cycle or other hormonal shifts, typically clustering along the jaw, chin and neck, and often deeper and more tender than surface congestion. Since hormones are involved in essentially all acne, the phrase describes a pattern rather than a cause. Since hormones are involved in essentially all acne, the phrase describes a pattern rather than a cause. Persistent, painful or scarring acne is a medical matter, and topical skincare works at a level the driver simply is not at.

The pattern people are describing

Lesions along the lower third of the face — jawline, chin, upper neck — that flare in a predictable relationship to the cycle, feel sore before they surface, and last longer than a surface whitehead. It commonly appears or persists in adulthood, particularly in women, and it can coexist with otherwise unremarkable skin. The pattern is real and worth noticing. What it does not tell you is whether hormone levels are abnormal; most people with this pattern have entirely normal bloodwork, which is a point clinicians make often.

Why topical routines plateau

Surface actives work on the surface. A BHA can help the look of congestion, niacinamide can help the appearance of tone and oiliness, azelaic acid is well liked for the appearance of both bumps and marks — all cosmetic contributions, and all of them useful. None of them alter androgen signalling in the sebaceous gland, and deep lesions form well below where a serum reaches. That is why people describe hitting a ceiling: the routine is doing its job, the job is just smaller than the problem.

A routine that at least does no harm

Barrier first. Over-stripping is the classic escalation — harsher cleanser, more acid, more spot treatment — and it produces irritated skin with the same acne on it. Use a gentle low-pH cleanser, one active introduced at a time, a light non-comedogenic moisturiser, and daily sunscreen so post-acne marks fade rather than darken. Give any change eight to twelve weeks before judging it. Hydrocolloid patches on surfaced spots are fine; picking is what turns a two-week spot into a six-month mark.

What warrants a doctor

Deep painful lesions, scarring, acne that has not responded to sensible over-the-counter care, or acne causing real distress — all reasons to book. Sudden severe onset, or adult acne appearing alongside irregular periods, unusual hair growth or scalp hair thinning, should be raised with a doctor rather than a shop assistant, since the assessment there goes beyond skin. Bring a photo timeline and a list of what you have already tried and for how long; it makes the appointment substantially more useful.
