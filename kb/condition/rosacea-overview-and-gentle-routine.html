Rosacea: what the term covers, and how calming skincare fits around treatment

⏱ 2 min read

The patterns, described generally

Clinicians describe rosacea by what dominates. One pattern is flushing and persistent central-face redness with visible small vessels. Another adds inflamed bumps and pustules, which is why it is so often mistaken for adult acne — though rosacea characteristically lacks blackheads. A third involves gradual thickening of skin, most often on the nose. A fourth affects the eyes, causing grittiness, dryness or lid inflammation, and it can appear without much skin change at all. People often overlap categories, and the pattern is what determines a doctor's approach.

Triggers are personal, so keep a diary

Commonly reported provocations include sun, heat, cold wind, hot drinks, alcohol, spicy food, exercise, stress, saunas and abrupt temperature swings. None of these apply to everyone, and lists copied off the internet lead to pointless restriction. A fortnight of noting what preceded a flush usually identifies two or three real culprits, which is far more useful than avoiding everything. Korean bathhouse culture and 찜질방 (jjimjilbang) heat rooms are worth approaching cautiously if heat is one of yours.

A 진정 routine that does not provoke it

진정 (jinjeong) means calming, and it is the entire brief here. Keep the routine short: a non-foaming lukewarm cleanse, one soothing fragrance-free moisturiser, sunscreen every morning. Centella, madecassoside and panthenol are the sensible picks — they help the appearance of redness and support a comfortable barrier, which is a cosmetic contribution, not a clinical one. Skip scrubs, high-strength acids, strong retinoids, alcohol-heavy toners, menthol and hot water. If organic UV filters sting, a mineral or hybrid Korean sunscreen is usually better tolerated. Green colour-correcting primer is camouflage, nothing more.

Why a clinician, and why early

Rosacea is diagnosed clinically, and the things it resembles — seborrhoeic dermatitis, perioral dermatitis, contact reactions, lupus-related rashes — are managed very differently. Getting the label right is the whole game. Raise eye symptoms explicitly, because ocular involvement is under-recognised and is not something to treat with skincare. Vessel changes and skin thickening tend to progress if left, which is a practical argument for seeing someone early rather than cycling through calming serums for two years first.

What is the 10-step Korean skincare routine?

The 10-step Korean skincare routine is a layered framework popularized in Korea: oil cleanser, water cleanser, exfoliant, toner, essence, serum or ampoule, sheet mask, eye cream, moisturizer and sunscreen. It's a customizable menu, not a daily mandate, and most people use only the steps their skin needs.

What is a good Korean skincare routine for beginners?

A simple beginner Korean routine is a gentle cleanser, a hydrating toner, a lightweight moisturizer and daily sunscreen, adding one targeted serum once your skin adjusts. Introduce actives slowly, one at a time, and patch-test, following the Korean principle of gentle, barrier-friendly care over many steps at once.

How often should you exfoliate in a Korean routine?

Once or twice a week for most people, up to three times for oily and resilient skin. Frequency matters far more than strength — a mild acid used consistently beats a strong one that forces you to stop.
