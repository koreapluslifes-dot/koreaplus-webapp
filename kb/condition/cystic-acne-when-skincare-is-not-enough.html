Cystic and nodular acne: deep painful lesions that need medical care

⏱ 2 min read
⚡ Quick answer: Deep, painful lumps that sit under the skin for weeks and heal leaving a scar are nodular or cystic acne, and this is the point at which over-the-counter skincare stops being the appropriate tool. Scarring is permanent and largely preventable with timely medical treatment, so delay is the actual risk here. See a dermatologist early, and let gentle skincare work around that care rather than substituting for it.

What makes it different

Ordinary acne sits near the surface. Nodules and cysts form deep in the dermis, where inflammation involves the whole follicle and the tissue around it. They are firm or fluctuant, tender to touch, often larger than a pea, and they persist for weeks rather than days. Several may cluster and connect. They rarely come to a head, which is why the usual instincts fail. The pain itself is diagnostic information — surface acne does not throb, and something that does deserves an appointment.

The scarring argument for not waiting

Deep inflammation damages the surrounding structure, and once the tissue remodels, the resulting depression or raised scar is permanent. Treating scars afterwards is slower, costlier and less satisfying than preventing them. That is the single strongest reason to see a doctor early rather than working through a queue of serums first. Korea's 피부과 (pibugwa) dermatology clinics are unusually accessible, and if you are in the country the barrier to being seen is low. Elsewhere, ask for the referral sooner than feels necessary.

What not to do

Do not squeeze, needle, lance or attempt drainage. Deep lesions have no exit route and pressure pushes inflammation sideways into surrounding tissue, which enlarges the eventual scar. Skip high-strength acids and aggressive scrubs on actively inflamed skin. Be sceptical of the purging excuse: purging is a real short-term effect of some actives, but months of worsening deep lesions is not purging, it is a signal to stop and get assessed. Do not stack multiple strong actives out of frustration.

The supporting routine

Keep it deliberately boring while under medical care. A gentle non-stripping cleanser twice daily, a light non-comedogenic moisturiser — treatment often makes skin dry and sensitive, and moisturiser makes that tolerable — and daily broad-spectrum sunscreen, because post-inflammatory marks darken with UV and outlast the acne itself by months. Hydrocolloid patches suit surfaced lesions only. Fragrance-free, minimal, consistent. This is barrier support and comfort while the actual treatment does the work.

Is Korean skincare good for acne?

Korean skincare can suit blemish-prone skin through gentle, barrier-friendly care using ingredients like centella, heartleaf, niacinamide and BHA. The approach favors soothing over stripping, since over-cleansing can worsen oil and breakouts. Persistent or severe acne should be assessed by a dermatologist rather than self-treated.

Do you really need all 10 steps of Korean skincare?

No. The 10-step routine is a menu, not a requirement. Many Koreans use a simple core of cleanser, hydrating toner, moisturizer and sunscreen, adding serums, essences or masks only as their skin needs. The point is consistency and customization, not maximum steps.

Do I really need to double cleanse?

At night, if you wear sunscreen or makeup — yes. Water-based cleansers cannot break down water-resistant sunscreen, so a single cleanse leaves a film behind. In the morning, one gentle cleanse or just water is plenty.

What is a first essence and do I need one?

A first essence is a watery, high-percentage ferment applied straight after cleansing, before anything else. It is a hydration and conditioning step. Pleasant and popular, but genuinely optional.
