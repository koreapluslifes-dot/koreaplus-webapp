Psoriasis: an immune condition where moisturiser is comfort, not treatment

⏱ 2 min read
⚡ Quick answer: Psoriasis is a chronic immune-mediated condition in which skin cells turn over far too quickly, producing thickened, sharply defined plaques with silvery scale, classically on elbows, knees, scalp and lower back. It is a medical diagnosis with medical treatment, and no cosmetic product treats it. Emollients do make plaques more comfortable and less prone to painful cracking, which is a genuine contribution — as comfort care alongside a clinician's plan, never instead of one.

How it differs from eczema

건선 (geonseon) plaques tend to be well-demarcated, with a clear edge between affected and normal skin, and carry thick silvery-white scale. Eczema is typically less defined, more intensely itchy, and favours the creases rather than the extensor surfaces. Psoriasis also produces recognisable nail changes — pitting, thickening, separation — and can affect the scalp beyond the hairline. It is systemic inflammation expressed in skin, not a surface problem, which is why topical cosmetics cannot reach it. Only a clinician should make this call.

Mention any joint symptoms, promptly

A proportion of people with psoriasis develop inflammatory joint disease, and joint damage from it can be permanent. Stiffness lasting longer than half an hour in the morning, swollen or painful joints, swollen fingers or toes, or persistent heel and lower-back pain should be raised with a doctor without waiting for the next routine appointment. This is the single most important thing on this page. Skin severity does not reliably predict joint involvement, so mild plaques are no reassurance at all.

What emollients actually do

They soften scale, reduce cracking and fissuring, ease the tight painful feeling, and make plaques less visually obvious — all real quality-of-life gains, all cosmetic. Use thick, bland, fragrance-free creams or ointments generously and often, especially after lukewarm bathing while skin is still damp. Soften scale rather than picking or scraping it: skin injury can provoke new plaques at the site of trauma, a well-described phenomenon, which makes scrubbing genuinely counterproductive. Avoid fragrance, alcohol-heavy toners and hot water.

Be sceptical of what is sold to you

A cosmetic product may lawfully describe helping the appearance of dryness, flaking or roughness. It may not treat psoriasis, and any product implying otherwise is over-claiming. Unregulated creams marketed for psoriasis have repeatedly been found by regulators in various countries to contain undeclared steroids, which is a real hazard rather than a theoretical one. Buy from legitimate channels, keep the medical plan with the doctor who wrote it, and treat the shelf as a source of comfort only.
