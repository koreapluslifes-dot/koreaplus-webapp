Some By Mi AHA BHA PHA 30 Days Miracle Serum

⏱ 2 min read
⚡ Quick answer: AHA BHA PHA 30 Days Miracle Serum is Some By Mi's serum, built around AHA (Glycolic/Lactic) and BHA (Salicylic) and PHA (Gluconolactone) and Tea Tree. Oily, combination and congested skin that wants a mild everyday exfoliating step rather than a weekly strong one.

The serum in the brand's best-known range: three acid families at cosmetic levels alongside tea tree water, aimed at oily and congested skin. The three-acid approach is more about covering different solubilities than about strength — the AHA works on the surface, the BHA is oil-soluble enough to get into a pore, the PHA is the gentlest of the set. The whole line is built around the same promise and sold as a set.

🏷️ Some By Mi 🧪 Serum

What it is built around

🍋 AHA (Glycolic/Lactic)🧽 BHA (Salicylic)🫧 PHA (Gluconolactone)🌱 Tea Tree

Who it suits

Oily, combination and congested skin that wants a mild everyday exfoliating step rather than a weekly strong one. Reasonable for the look of rough texture and visible pores. Dry, sensitive or barrier-compromised skin should stay away, and so should anyone already using a nightly retinoid. If you have inflamed cystic breakouts, this is not the product and a clinician is the better call.

🔴 Acne & breakouts🪨 Rough & uneven texture🕳️ Pores & texture⚫ Blackheads & sebaceous filaments

How to use it

Evening, after toner, before moisturiser. Start twice a week and build slowly, watching for tightness or stinging. Follow with a plain moisturiser and use sunscreen daily — exfoliating acids make that non-negotiable. Do not run the toner, cleanser and serum from this line all at once; pick one exfoliating step and keep the rest bland.

⚠️ Worth knowing: The 30 days in the name is a marketing promise no leave-on cosmetic can make good on, and the miracle framing sets people up to over-use the whole range. Buying the matching cleanser, toner, serum and cream is the most common route to an irritated, stripped barrier we see in this category. Tea tree is also a known contact allergen for a minority.

What is the difference between AHA and BHA?

AHAs like glycolic and lactic acid are water-soluble and work on the skin's surface to brighten and smooth, while BHA like salicylic acid is oil-soluble and gets into pores. In Korean skincare both are used sparingly, a few times weekly, always with daily sun protection.

What is the difference between essence and serum?

In Korean skincare, essence is typically a thinner, hydration-focused layer that preps skin, while serum is more concentrated with targeted actives like vitamin C or niacinamide. Essence usually comes first; serum follows. The line between them blurs, and not every routine needs both.

What is BHA and what does it do?

BHA, usually salicylic acid, is an oil-soluble exfoliant that can penetrate pores to dissolve oil and dead skin, helping refine the look of texture and blemish-prone skin. In Korean routines it's used a few times weekly, with sunscreen by day, to avoid over-exfoliating.

What is the difference between essence, serum and ampoule?

Essence is the lightest and most hydration-focused, serum is more concentrated and targeted, and ampoule is the most concentrated of the three. The boundaries are marketing convention, not regulated definitions.
