COSRX The Vitamin C 23 Serum

⏱ 2 min read
⚡ Quick answer: The Vitamin C 23 Serum is COSRX's serum, built around Vitamin C. For normal, oily or combination skin that already tolerates actives and wants the strongest well-documented form of vitamin C rather than a comfortable derivative.

A high-strength pure L-ascorbic acid serum in a thick, slightly syrupy base, and the strongest rung of COSRX's vitamin C ladder — the same line runs a gentler low-percentage version for people who cannot take this one. Pure ascorbic acid is the best-evidenced form of topical vitamin C for the look of tone and brightness, but it is also the most unstable and the most likely to sting. Nothing about this formula hides that.

🏷️ COSRX 🧪 Serum

What it is built around

🍊 Vitamin C

Who it suits

For normal, oily or combination skin that already tolerates actives and wants the strongest well-documented form of vitamin C rather than a comfortable derivative. Good for the look of uneven tone, dullness and lingering post-blemish marks. Sensitive, rosacea-prone or barrier-compromised skin should not start here — the lower-strength version of the same serum exists for exactly that reason.

🟤 Dark spots & tone🌟 Dullness & glow🩹 Post-acne marks (PIE & PIH)☀️ Sun damage & photoageing

How to use it

Morning, on clean dry skin, before anything else — a few drops is plenty and more is genuinely wasteful. Wait a minute, then moisturiser and sunscreen. Sunscreen is not optional with this one; vitamin C is a poor substitute for it and a good partner to it. Start two or three mornings a week and build up only if your skin stays calm.

⚠️ Worth knowing: It stings for most people on the first applications, and a burn that does not settle within a few minutes means stop, not persevere. Ascorbic acid oxidises: as the serum turns from pale to amber it is losing potency, so buy small and finish it. Do not layer it with strong exfoliating acids or a retinoid in the same session. The tacky finish also pills under some sunscreens.

What is the difference between essence and serum?

In Korean skincare, essence is typically a thinner, hydration-focused layer that preps skin, while serum is more concentrated with targeted actives like vitamin C or niacinamide. Essence usually comes first; serum follows. The line between them blurs, and not every routine needs both.

Can you use vitamin C and niacinamide together?

Yes, current understanding is that vitamin C and niacinamide can be used together safely, and many Korean formulas combine them. The old warning that they cancel out is largely outdated. If your skin is sensitive, you can still apply them at different times of day to be cautious.

Can I use vitamin C and retinol together?

Not in the same layer, but easily in the same routine — vitamin C in the morning, retinol at night. That split suits both: vitamin C partners with sunscreen, and retinoids are degraded by light.

What is the difference between essence, serum and ampoule?

Essence is the lightest and most hydration-focused, serum is more concentrated and targeted, and ampoule is the most concentrated of the three. The boundaries are marketing convention, not regulated definitions.
