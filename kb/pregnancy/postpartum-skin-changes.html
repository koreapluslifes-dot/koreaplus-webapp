Skin after birth: what changes and when to reintroduce actives

⏱ 1 min read
⚡ Quick answer: Postpartum skin commonly shows dryness, hair shedding, lingering melasma and sometimes a return of acne as hormones resettle. Guidance on which actives are appropriate while breastfeeding differs from pregnancy guidance and from clinician to clinician, so reintroduce in conversation with yours rather than on a schedule from the internet.

Postpartum skin commonly shows dryness, hair shedding, lingering melasma and sometimes a return of acne as hormones resettle. Guidance on which actives are appropriate while breastfeeding differs from pregnancy guidance and from clinician to clinician, so reintroduce in conversation with yours rather than on a schedule from the internet.

What commonly changes

Hormonal resettling can bring dryness, increased sensitivity, and noticeable hair shedding a few months after birth — the shedding is usually temporary. Melasma may begin to fade. Acne can return, sometimes in a different pattern than before pregnancy.

Reintroducing actives

Advice for breastfeeding is not identical to advice for pregnancy, and it varies. The general principle clinicians apply is minimal systemic absorption and keeping products away from areas an infant contacts. Ask about your specific list rather than assuming everything resumes at once.

Go slowly

Skin that has been on a simple routine for months will not appreciate three actives arriving in one week. Reintroduce one product at a time with two weeks between, which also tells you which one is responsible if something reacts.

Be realistic about the timeframe

This is the period of life least suited to a demanding routine. A gentle cleanser, a good moisturiser and sunscreen is a complete and entirely respectable routine, and it is the one most likely to actually get done.

🤍 Not medical advice. Cosmetic-ingredient guidance in pregnancy and breastfeeding varies between countries and between clinicians, and your situation is specific to you. Take your actual product list to your doctor or midwife and confirm it with them.
