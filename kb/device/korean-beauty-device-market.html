How Korea built the home beauty-device market

⏱ 1 min read
⚡ Quick answer: Korea's home-device category grew out of an unusually mainstream aesthetic-clinic culture — devices were positioned as at-home maintenance between appointments rather than as replacements for them. Combined with strong domestic electronics manufacturing, that produced a category in which Korean brands are among the fastest-growing and most visible worldwide — though the largest home-device makers by revenue are still Japanese, American and European.

Combined with strong domestic electronics manufacturing, that produced a category in which Korean brands are among the fastest-growing and most visible worldwide — though the largest home-device makers by revenue are still Japanese, American and European.

Clinic culture came first

Aesthetic procedures are far less stigmatised and far more routine in Korea than in most markets. A population already familiar with clinic treatments is a population that understands what a home device is imitating — and what it is not.

Manufacturing capability

Korea's consumer-electronics base meant the engineering, supply chain and design capability were already in place. Marrying that to a cosmetics industry that already knew how to sell efficacy was a short step.

Positioning as maintenance

The successful Korean framing has been between-appointment upkeep, not procedure replacement. That is both more honest and more durable than the promises the category makes elsewhere.

How to evaluate a device

Ask what mechanism it uses and whether that mechanism has evidence behind it, at roughly what intensity, and how often you would realistically use it. Devices that cannot answer the first question are selling you a light-up plastic object.
