Vitamin C: what the evidence says

⏱ 1 min read
⚡ Quick answer: Vitamin C brightens, evens tone and provides antioxidant defense under SPF. — Evidence grade: Strong. Vitamin C is a well-supported antioxidant cosmetic active. Pure L-ascorbic acid is most studied, typically used around 10-20% at a low pH (below ~3.5) for stability and uptake, and is best applied in the morning under sunscreen to support antioxidant defense and a brighter, more even-looking tone over time. It is prone to oxidation, so packaging and formulation matter. Gentler derivatives suit sensitive skin but have less direct evidence. Some users find L-ascorbic acid at high strength mildly irritating.

The claim: Vitamin C brightens, evens tone and provides antioxidant defense under SPF.

Strong evidence

What the research actually shows

Vitamin C is a well-supported antioxidant cosmetic active. Pure L-ascorbic acid is most studied, typically used around 10-20% at a low pH (below ~3.5) for stability and uptake, and is best applied in the morning under sunscreen to support antioxidant defense and a brighter, more even-looking tone over time. It is prone to oxidation, so packaging and formulation matter. Gentler derivatives suit sensitive skin but have less direct evidence. Some users find L-ascorbic acid at high strength mildly irritating.

Can you use vitamin C and niacinamide together?

Yes, current understanding is that vitamin C and niacinamide can be used together safely, and many Korean formulas combine them. The old warning that they cancel out is largely outdated. If your skin is sensitive, you can still apply them at different times of day to be cautious.

Can I use vitamin C and retinol together?

Not in the same layer, but easily in the same routine — vitamin C in the morning, retinol at night. That split suits both: vitamin C partners with sunscreen, and retinoids are degraded by light.
