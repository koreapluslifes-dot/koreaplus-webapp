BB Cream

BB cream is a hybrid product that blends skincare, light coverage, and sun protection into one step. Its medical origins are German, but its transformation into a mass-market beauty staple happened largely in Korea.

Origin

BB cream is honestly best described as German-origin and Korea-popularized. The product is commonly traced to a German dermatologist, Dr. Christine Schrammek, who is credited with formulating a 'blemish balm' (beauty balm) around the 1960s to protect and cover skin after facial peels and surgery. The format was later embraced and reworked by Korean cosmetics companies, who repositioned it from a clinical aftercare product into an everyday all-in-one base, and from there it spread internationally.

How it works

A typical BB cream combines a moisturizing base, light-to-medium tinted coverage, and usually sun protection into a single emulsion. The aim is to even out skin tone and provide a natural finish while reducing the number of separate products a person applies. Formulas vary widely, so the exact balance of skincare, coverage, and SPF differs by brand and product.

BB versus CC

BB cream inspired related categories, most notably CC ('color correcting' or 'complexion correcting') cream, which generally emphasizes neutralizing redness or dullness with a lighter, more adjustable finish. Both belong to the broader family of multitasking 'do-it-all' bases that K-beauty helped make mainstream.

Key facts
  • Commonly traced to German dermatology (often credited to Dr. Christine Schrammek, around the 1960s)
  • Originally used to cover and soothe skin after procedures
  • Popularized as everyday makeup by Korean brands
  • Combines moisturizer, tint, and usually SPF in one step
  • Inspired related formats such as CC cream
