The 10-Step Routine

⏱ 2 min read
⚡ Quick answer: The 10-step Korean skincare routine: a layered, customizable sequence from cleansing to moisturizing and sun protection. What it is and how flexible it is.

The '10-step routine' is a popularized framework for layering Korean skincare products in a deliberate order. It is best understood as a flexible template rather than a strict rulebook.

What the routine is

The 10-step routine is a commonly cited sequence that often runs roughly as: oil cleanser, water-based cleanser, exfoliant, toner, essence, serum or ampoule, sheet mask, eye cream, moisturizer, and sun protection. The exact list varies by source. Its rise in the English-speaking world is often linked to K-beauty marketing and writing in the mid-2010s, which packaged the layering philosophy into an easy-to-grasp number.

The logic of layering

The underlying idea is to apply products from thinnest to thickest, building hydration and targeted treatments in stages and sealing them with moisturizer and, in daytime, sunscreen. Rather than depending on one or two products, the routine spreads functions across many lightweight layers, which is central to the K-beauty approach.

Flexibility and reality

Despite the memorable name, few people perform all ten steps every day, and many Korean consumers use far fewer. Steps such as masking or exfoliating are typically occasional, and the routine is meant to be customized to skin type, season, and time. It is fair to present '10 steps' as a popularized concept and marketing-friendly framing, not a medical prescription.

Key facts
  • A layered sequence, often listed as around ten steps
  • Common order runs from cleansing through treatments to moisturizer and SPF
  • Built on applying products thinnest to thickest
  • Popularized in the West largely through mid-2010s K-beauty media
  • Meant to be customized; few do all ten steps daily

What is a good Korean skincare routine for beginners?

A simple beginner Korean routine is a gentle cleanser, a hydrating toner, a lightweight moisturizer and daily sunscreen, adding one targeted serum once your skin adjusts. Introduce actives slowly, one at a time, and patch-test, following the Korean principle of gentle, barrier-friendly care over many steps at once.

How often should you exfoliate in a Korean routine?

Once or twice a week for most people, up to three times for oily and resilient skin. Frequency matters far more than strength — a mild acid used consistently beats a strong one that forces you to stop.
