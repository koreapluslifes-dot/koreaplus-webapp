Your retinoid is too much: how to back off without abandoning it

⏱ 2 min read
⚡ Quick answer: Redness, flaking, stinging and tightness in the first weeks of a retinoid are common and usually a frequency problem rather than a reason to stop entirely. Reduce to twice a week, apply a moisturiser before and after, use a pea-sized amount on a dry face, and build back slowly over months. Persistent severe irritation, or anything with a prescription retinoid, is a conversation for the prescribing doctor.

What is happening, and what 'retinisation' is worth

Retinoids accelerate skin cell turnover, and the early weeks often bring dryness, flaking, redness and sensitivity while the surface adjusts. The industry calls this retinisation and treats it as a rite of passage. That framing is useful up to a point and abused past it — it has persuaded a great many people to push through reactions that were simply too much product too often. Discomfort is not evidence that it is working, and there is no prize for enduring it. Efficacy comes from consistent use over months, not from intensity.

The adjustments that usually rescue it

Drop to twice a week and hold there for a month before increasing. Use a genuinely pea-sized amount for the whole face. Apply to completely dry skin, since damp skin increases penetration and irritation. Sandwich it: moisturiser, retinoid, moisturiser. Keep it off the eyelids, the corners of the nose and the lip edges, which react first. Stop all acids and vitamin C while you settle, use them on alternate nights afterwards, and wear sunscreen every morning without exception, which matters more on a retinoid than off one.

Strength, form and what Korean shelves offer

Retinol, retinal and retinyl esters differ in how directly they act, with retinal generally regarded as more potent than retinol at comparable percentages, and esters as gentler and correspondingly less effective. Encapsulated forms are marketed as gentler and there is reasonable rationale for that, though the difference is easy to overstate. Korean formulations often pair a lower-strength retinoid with hydrating and calming ingredients, which suits people who found a Western high-percentage serum unmanageable. Bakuchiol is frequently offered as an alternative; the evidence base for it is much smaller and it is not a like-for-like substitute.

When to stop and ask someone

Stop and see a doctor if you develop persistent swelling, blistering, weeping, severe pain, or irritation that does not settle within a few days of stopping. If you are using a prescription retinoid such as tretinoin, any adjustment belongs with the doctor who prescribed it rather than with an article. Anyone pregnant, trying to conceive or breastfeeding should discuss retinoid use with a clinician before continuing — this is a well-established area of medical caution and not one to resolve by reading forums.
