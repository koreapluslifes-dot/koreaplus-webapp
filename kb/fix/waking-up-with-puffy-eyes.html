Puffy eyes in the morning: what causes them and what actually helps

⏱ 2 min read
⚡ Quick answer: Lying flat for hours lets fluid settle in the thin tissue around the eyes, and salt, alcohol, crying, allergies and poor sleep all add to it. Most morning puffiness resolves on its own within an hour or two of being upright. Cold and elevation help genuinely; caffeine eye creams offer a modest, temporary change in appearance at best. Sudden one-sided swelling, pain or vision changes need urgent medical attention.

Why mornings are worst

The skin around the eye is thin and the tissue beneath it holds fluid readily. Horizontal for seven or eight hours, fluid distributes differently than it does when you are upright, and the periorbital area shows it first. Standing up reverses it gradually, which is why puffiness that alarms you at seven has usually softened by nine. Sleeping face-down or on one side tends to make it more pronounced, and more asymmetric, which is worth knowing before you conclude something is wrong with one eye.

The contributors you can influence

A salty dinner, alcohol the night before, crying, short or broken sleep, and allergies — which add itching and rubbing to fluid retention, and rubbing makes everything worse. Nasal congestion from a cold does it too. Sleeping with your head slightly raised reduces it for many people. So does a genuinely cold compress for a few minutes: chilled spoons, a cold flannel, a gel mask from the fridge. The cooling constricts vessels briefly and the effect is real, modest and temporary.

What eye products can and cannot do

Caffeine is the most commonly used ingredient here and there is some support for a short-term change in the appearance of puffiness, largely through vasoconstriction. The effect is modest, temporary and easily matched by a cold compress. Products containing film formers can tighten the look of the area for a few hours. What none of them do is alter the underlying anatomy. Under-eye bags that are present regardless of sleep, salt or time of day generally relate to fat pad and tissue changes with age, and no cream addresses that — the honest options there are medical, and belong with a clinician.

When puffiness is a medical matter

Get urgent medical attention for sudden swelling of one eye, swelling with pain, redness, fever or any change in vision, or swelling that accompanies swelling of the lips or tongue or difficulty breathing. See a doctor for persistent puffiness that does not settle through the day, swelling that appeared with a new medication, or puffiness alongside changes in weight, urination or fatigue. Periorbital swelling occasionally reflects thyroid, kidney, cardiac or allergic conditions, and none of that is a skincare question.
