Skin suddenly oilier than it used to be: what actually drives that

⏱ 2 min read

Weather does most of the work

Sebum production increases as skin surface temperature rises, which is why the same face can be matte in February and shining by lunchtime in August. Humidity compounds it: sweat mixes with sebum and spreads it across the surface, so the film looks and feels greater than it is. Korean summers, hot and heavily humid, are why the local market carries such an extensive range of gel textures, sebum-controlling toners and blotting products between June and September. If your oiliness arrived with the season, that is very likely your whole explanation.

The rebound-oil myth, examined

You will read constantly that stripping your skin makes it produce more oil in retaliation. It is an appealing story and the evidence for it is thin. What harsh cleansing demonstrably does is disturb the surface, which leaves skin feeling tight and flaky while the sebum that is produced sits on a rougher surface and reads as greasier. That is a real problem worth solving with a gentler cleanser. It is just not the feedback loop it is usually described as, and the distinction matters because it changes what you do about it.

Causes worth taking seriously

Hormonal shifts — puberty, cycle phase, pregnancy, coming off or starting hormonal contraception, perimenopause — genuinely change sebum output, and that is a medical context rather than a product one. Several medications do too. Some people find rich occlusive creams leave a residual film they read as their own oil. Stress and sleep debt are commonly reported, though the research linking them to measured sebum is less firm than the internet implies. If the change is abrupt, marked and unexplained, a doctor is a better first stop than a new toner.

What actually helps day to day

Cleanse twice daily with something mild rather than something aggressive. Keep hydrating — 겉기름 속건조 (geotgireum sokgeonjo), oily on the surface and dry underneath, is a Korean shorthand for a genuinely common state, and skipping moisturiser makes it worse rather than better. Use gel or fluid textures in warm months and save the cream for winter. Blot rather than re-powder. Niacinamide has reasonable support for the appearance of oiliness and pore visibility, though the effect is modest and slow, and anyone promising matte skin in a week is selling.
