Why your skincare pills, and how to stop it happening

⏱ 2 min read
⚡ Quick answer: Pilling is friction, not your skin rejecting anything. Film-forming ingredients — silicones, gums, high-molecular-weight hyaluronic acid, various polymers and the resins in sunscreen — sit on the surface rather than sinking in, and roll into tiny threads when a later layer or your fingertips drag across them. Fewer layers, less product per layer, and pressing instead of rubbing fixes the large majority of cases.

What is actually rolling off your face

Those little grey-white worms are almost never dead skin, whatever the internet says. They are the product itself: a film former that has partially dried, then been sheared by a second film former arriving on top of it. Korean routines are unusually prone to this because layering is the whole idea, and because texture-enhancing polymers are cheap, pleasant and everywhere. Korean reviewers call it 밀림 (millim), literally pushing or shoving, which is a more accurate description than anything in English. Nothing has gone wrong with your skin. Something has gone wrong with the stack.

The ingredients that most often cause it

Look for carbomer, xanthan gum, cellulose gums, sodium polyacrylate, PEG compounds, dimethicone and its heavier cousins, and high-molecular-weight sodium hyaluronate. Sunscreens are frequent offenders because the film that gives you protection is by definition designed to stay on the surface. Sheet mask essences and thick hydrating toners full of gums are another common trigger. None of these is a bad ingredient. They simply do not all get along, and Korean shoppers have a word for exactly this problem: 화장품 궁합 (hwajangpum gunghap), the compatibility between two products.

What to change, in order

Cut the number of layers before you cut any single product. Three well-chosen steps pill far less than seven thin ones. Use less of each thing — most people apply toner and essence far more heavily than needed. Press products in with flat palms rather than rubbing in circles. Give each layer thirty seconds or so to settle before the next, and give sunscreen longer than that. If pilling only starts when one specific product enters the sequence, move it earlier, move it later, or accept that it does not belong in this routine.

What pilling does not tell you

It says nothing about whether a product is working, and nothing about whether your skin is absorbing anything. Absorption is not the goal for most of what you apply — a moisturiser is meant to sit at the surface, and a sunscreen absolutely must. Pilling also does not mean a product is badly made. Some of the most pleasant single-use textures in Korean skincare are heavy on gums, and they behave impeccably alone. The failure is combinatorial. Test products in isolation before you write any of them off.
