⚡ Quick answer: What a Korean sunscreen actually is and whether you need one. The final morning step — filters UV so that everything else in your routine is not quietly being undone — a fluid, gel-cream or milky lotion texture, used am, always last before makeup.

At a glance

Texture: Fluid, gel-cream or milky lotion ≈ Facial SPF / daily sunscreen When: AM, always last before makeup

Do you need it?

essential Nothing else in a routine comes close. Daily broad-spectrum SPF is the single best-evidenced anti-ageing and anti-pigmentation step available.

⏭️ You can skip it if: Never for daytime. At night it serves no purpose at all.
💡 Tip: Korean sunscreens are famous for elegant textures because Korea permits modern UV filters that some markets do not — that is a formulation advantage, not marketing.

🙋 People also ask

Why is Korean sunscreen considered better?

Korean sunscreens are often praised for elegant, lightweight textures that feel pleasant enough to wear daily, which encourages consistent use. They frequently use newer filters approved in Korea but not the US. Preferences are partly cosmetic; effectiveness depends on applying enough and reapplying regardless of brand.

Do you really need all 10 steps of Korean skincare?

No. The 10-step routine is a menu, not a requirement. Many Koreans use a simple core of cleanser, hydrating toner, moisturizer and sunscreen, adding serums, essences or masks only as their skin needs. The point is consistency and customization, not maximum steps.

Why is Korean sunscreen better?

Because Korea approves modern UV filters — Uvinul A Plus, Tinosorb S and M among them — that give strong UVA protection in light, non-greasy textures. Some markets, notably the US, have not approved them.

Do I really need to double cleanse?

At night, if you wear sunscreen or makeup — yes. Water-based cleansers cannot break down water-resistant sunscreen, so a single cleanse leaves a film behind. In the morning, one gentle cleanse or just water is plenty.
