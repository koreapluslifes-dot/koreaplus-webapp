Witch Hazel (Hamamelis) in spring: how to adjust

⏱ 1 min read
⚡ Quick answer: Using Witch Hazel (Hamamelis) in spring: the season brings rising UV and pollen-season reactivity, so what changes is texture, frequency and what you layer around it — not usually whether you use it at all.

Using Witch Hazel (Hamamelis) in spring: the season brings rising UV and pollen-season reactivity, so what changes is texture, frequency and what you layer around it — not usually whether you use it at all.

The tightening sensation is real and almost entirely temporary. Witch hazel's tannins constrict the skin surface for a while, which reads as smaller pores in a mirror. The bigger question is the distillate: many versions carry alcohol, and on already-reactive skin that combination gives you redness dressed up as freshness.

What spring does to your skin

Fluctuating temperatures, pollen and sensitivity, and rising UV mean a gentle, barrier-supportive routine with reliable daily SPF.

How that changes Witch Hazel (Hamamelis)

Spring is when UV climbs while skin is still adjusting out of winter — keep the barrier steady and let sunscreen do the heavy lifting. Witch Hazel (Hamamelis) works on oil control, pores & texture, redness & sensitivity, and it is normally applied BOTH.
