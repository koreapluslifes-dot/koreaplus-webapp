Does the sauna help your skin? The genuine answer

⏱ 1 min read
⚡ Quick answer: Heat increases blood flow and produces an immediate flush that looks like radiance, and the humidity is pleasant. It does not detoxify skin or clean pores — sweat glands and pores are separate structures. For rosacea-prone skin and melasma, heat is a documented trigger.

Heat increases blood flow and produces an immediate flush that looks like radiance, and the humidity is pleasant. It does not detoxify skin or clean pores — sweat glands and pores are separate structures. For rosacea-prone skin and melasma, heat is a documented trigger.

What heat does

It dilates surface blood vessels, producing the post-sauna flush people read as glow. It also relaxes you, which is not nothing. Both effects are real and both are temporary.

The detox claim

Sweat is produced by sweat glands; oil and pore contents come from sebaceous glands. Sweating does not flush out your pores, and the body's actual detoxification runs through the liver and kidneys. Enjoy the sauna for what it is.

Who should be careful

Heat is a well-documented trigger for rosacea-prone flushing and for melasma, which responds to heat as well as to UV. If either applies to you, shorter sessions and cooler rooms are the sensible adjustment.

Afterwards

Rinse, and moisturise while skin is still damp. Prolonged heat and humidity followed by dry air is dehydrating, which is why the post-bathhouse moisturising step is treated as automatic in Korea.
