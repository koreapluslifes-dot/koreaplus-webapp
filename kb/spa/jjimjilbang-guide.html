Jjimjilbang explained: what happens inside a Korean bathhouse

⏱ 1 min read
⚡ Quick answer: A jjimjilbang is a Korean bathhouse combining gender-separated bathing areas with a mixed-gender common floor of heated rooms, sleeping areas and food. It is an ordinary, family-friendly institution rather than a luxury spa, and it is where Korea's skincare culture is most visible.

A jjimjilbang is a Korean bathhouse combining gender-separated bathing areas with a mixed-gender common floor of heated rooms, sleeping areas and food. It is an ordinary, family-friendly institution rather than a luxury spa, and it is where Korea's skincare culture is most visible.

The layout

You leave shoes at the entrance, receive a locker key and a uniform, and the building splits into two parts: gender-separated wet areas with pools and showers where nobody wears clothing, and a mixed common area where everyone wears the issued uniform.

The etiquette that matters

Shower thoroughly before entering any pool — this is non-negotiable and the most common mistake visitors make. Nudity in the bathing area is standard and unremarkable. Keep your voice down, and note that visible tattoos are still restricted at some establishments.

The heated rooms

The common floor holds rooms lined with different materials — salt, charcoal, jade, clay — each held at a different temperature. Move between them, drink water, and leave when you have had enough rather than enduring.

Why it belongs in a skincare library

Bathing culture is where Korean skin habits are learned: thorough cleansing, the sensation of properly hydrated skin, and moisturising afterwards as a matter of course. The routine did not come from marketing; it came from here.
