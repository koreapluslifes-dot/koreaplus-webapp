The small rough bumps on upper arms and thighs are extremely common and generally harmless. They do not scrub away — the useful approach is chemical rather than physical: urea or lactic acid to soften, plus consistent moisturising. Improvement is real but gradual, and it returns if you stop.

What it is

Keratin builds up around hair follicles, creating small firm bumps with a sandpaper feel, often with some redness. It is very common, frequently runs in families, and is a cosmetic concern rather than a health one.

Why scrubbing fails

The build-up sits around and within the follicle, not loose on the surface. Physical scrubbing irritates the surrounding skin and adds redness without reaching the actual plug — which is why the bumps look angrier after aggressive exfoliation.

What actually helps

Urea at higher percentages softens the keratin, and lactic or salicylic acid loosens it. Applied consistently alongside a good moisturiser, texture improves over weeks. It is management rather than cure — stop, and it gradually comes back.

Be patient and gentle

Expect gradual change over a couple of months. Escalating strength because week two looks the same is how people end up with irritated, still-bumpy arms.
