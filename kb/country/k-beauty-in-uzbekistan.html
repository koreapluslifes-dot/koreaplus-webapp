K-Beauty in Uzbekistan: Koryo-saram History Meets a Young Market

A community that predates the trend

Uzbekistan's Koryo-saram, ethnic Koreans descended from communities deported from the Soviet Far East in 1937, have been part of the country for generations. That history has kept Korean food, language and cultural ties present in Uzbek life long before anyone used the word Hallyu. Layer on top of it the substantial number of Uzbeks who have worked in South Korea and returned, plus the ordinary reach of Korean drama and music among younger people, and you have a market where Korean products need no introduction at all.

Bazaars, marketplaces and small importers

Tashkent has the widest choice, across shopping centres, cosmetics shops and the bazaars, with regional online marketplaces that have expanded into Uzbekistan carrying a good deal of the rest. Small importers fill the gaps, and returning travellers fill more of them than anyone counts. Customs duties and periodically revised thresholds apply to parcels, so confirm the current rules before ordering in bulk. The persistent complaint on open marketplaces is authenticity, since a popular Korean product with wide name recognition is exactly what gets copied.

Continental extremes

Very hot dry summers, cold winters, and dust for a good part of the year. This is a climate that punishes the assumption that Korean routines are automatically transferable, because the lightest gel textures that suit a humid Seoul summer leave skin feeling tight in a Tashkent February. Layering a hydrating toner under a genuine emollient cream works far better. Cleansing oils and balms suit the dust, and calming formulas built on centella have an obvious use. Sheet masks are popular partly because they are an affordable treat.

What is difficult here

Labelling is a practical issue: regulated retail expects information in local languages and a lot of Korean export stock does not carry it, which pushes trade towards informal channels. Verifying that a widely counterfeited product is genuine is the main risk you are managing. Cushion refills are hard to source, and the freight cost on heavy items such as large toner bottles is enough to reshape what is worth buying. Prices land at a real multiple of Korean shelf prices, which is worth knowing before assuming a bargain.
