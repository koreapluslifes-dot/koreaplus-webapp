How K-Beauty Arrived and Grew in Germany

Germany, with its large and ingredient-conscious beauty market, embraced Korean skincare as part of the wider Korean Wave, drawn to its emphasis on hydration and gentle formulation.

An ingredient-aware market

German consumers are often noted for paying close attention to ingredients and formulations, and the drugstore (Drogerie) culture has long been central to everyday skincare shopping. This environment suited Korean beauty's reputation for innovative ingredients and a hydration-led philosophy. As Hallyu — the international spread of Korean pop culture — reached German audiences, particularly younger ones, interest in Korean skincare grew alongside it. There is a notable historical link, too: BB cream, a hero category in Korean beauty, originated in Germany before being widely popularized and refined in Korea.

What resonated locally

The gentle, layered approach associated with Korean routines appealed to consumers interested in barrier care and hydration. Ingredients commonly linked to K-beauty — centella asiatica, hyaluronic acid and various plant extracts — became familiar through reviews and beauty communities. Sheet masks and lightweight essences were popular entry points.

Where Germans buy it today

Korean beauty is available in Germany through drugstore and beauty retailers, general online marketplaces, and dedicated K-beauty online shops, some of which serve the broader European market. Specific brand availability changes over time. As with any imported cosmetics, shoppers should review ingredient lists and batch or expiry details and follow standard customs and shipping guidance when relevant.

Key facts
  • Germany's ingredient-conscious, drugstore-centric culture suited K-beauty.
  • BB cream originated in Germany before being popularized in Korea.
  • Hallyu drew younger German consumers to Korean skincare.
  • Hydration and barrier-care focus resonated locally.
  • K-beauty is sold via drugstores, online marketplaces and specialty shops.
