How K-Beauty Arrived and Grew in the Netherlands

The Netherlands, with its highly connected, English-fluent and e-commerce-friendly population, took to Korean beauty as part of the wider Korean Wave.

A digital-first uptake

Dutch consumers are highly active online and broadly comfortable shopping across borders within Europe, which made specialty Korean beauty accessible even before wide local retail presence. English-language beauty media and international communities reached Dutch audiences readily. As Hallyu — the global spread of Korean pop culture — grew among younger people, interest in Korean skincare and makeup expanded alongside K-pop and drama fandoms.

What resonated locally

The hydration-focused, gentle approach associated with Korean routines appealed to consumers interested in barrier care, fitting a climate that can be cool and changeable. Sheet masks, lightweight moisturizers and sunscreens were common entry points. Ingredients widely associated with K-beauty, such as centella asiatica and hyaluronic acid, became familiar to enthusiasts through reviews and recommendations.

Where the Dutch buy it today

Korean beauty is available in the Netherlands through drugstore and beauty retailers, general online marketplaces, and specialty K-beauty online shops, some serving the broader European market. Brand availability changes over time. As products are often imported, buyers should check ingredient and batch information and follow standard customs and shipping practices when ordering from outside the EU.

Key facts
  • Dutch consumers are highly active in cross-border online shopping.
  • English-language beauty media reached Dutch audiences readily.
  • Hydration and barrier-care focus suited the changeable climate.
  • Hallyu expanded interest among younger consumers.
  • K-beauty is sold via drugstores, online marketplaces and specialty shops.
