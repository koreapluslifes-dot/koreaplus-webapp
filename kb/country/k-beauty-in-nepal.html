K-Beauty in Nepal: Migration Ties, Kathmandu Shops and Mountain Air

⏱ 2 min read

People brought it, not distributors

Nepal is one of the main sending countries under South Korea's Employment Permit System, and Korean-language study for the entrance test is a familiar part of life for many young Nepalis. Large numbers of people have lived and worked in Korea and come back. They returned with products, with an understanding of what Korean routines actually look like day to day, and with brand preferences formed in Korean shops rather than from advertising. That is an unusually direct transmission route, and it is why Korean cosmetics feel less exotic here than in comparable markets.

Kathmandu, India and the grey market

Kathmandu carries by far the widest selection, in independent cosmetics shops and department stores, with much thinner availability elsewhere. A significant amount of stock arrives informally, either overland through India or with travellers, and formal import duties are high enough that this is unlikely to change soon. The consequence is a genuine counterfeit and old-stock problem. Check batch codes, be wary of sealed items priced far below what freight alone would cost, and prefer sellers who will tell you where a product came from.

Dust, dry winters and altitude

Kathmandu's air quality is poor for much of the year, particularly in winter, and the valley is dry and cold in the same season. That makes a proper cleansing step the most useful single habit, an oil or balm to lift the day off, followed by something gentle. Calming formulas, the 진정 (jinjeong) category, suit skin that looks reddened by dust and cold. Sun exposure at altitude is significant and sunscreen is widely under-used. None of this needs ten steps; three well-chosen products do more than a long routine bought piecemeal.

Price, in proportion

Set against local incomes, imported Korean skincare is a meaningful expense, and the multi-step routine that circulates online is a minority pursuit rather than a national habit. What is hard to find is the cheap, high-volume end of the Korean market, exactly the part that would suit the price point, because it cannot absorb freight and duty. Sun care is under-stocked. Refills are scarce. The realistic approach is a small number of items that each earn their place, bought from a source you can hold accountable.
