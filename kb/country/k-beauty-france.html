How K-Beauty Arrived and Grew in France

France, long regarded as a major center of beauty and skincare, welcomed Korean beauty as an innovative complement to its own strong cosmetics tradition, with adoption growing through the 2010s.

Two beauty cultures meet

France has a deep-rooted skincare and cosmetics heritage, including a strong pharmacy (parapharmacie) skincare tradition. Korean beauty entered this sophisticated market by offering novelty in textures, formats and ingredients. As Hallyu — the worldwide spread of Korean pop culture — gained French audiences, especially among younger consumers, curiosity about Korean skincare and makeup expanded. Major beauty retailers prominent in France helped bring selected Korean brands to a wide audience.

What appealed to French consumers

French shoppers, accustomed to a results-and-ritual approach to skincare, responded to the layered, hydration-focused philosophy associated with Korean routines. Innovative product formats — cushion compacts, sheet masks and lightweight essences — drew particular interest. Ingredients commonly linked to K-beauty, such as centella asiatica and hyaluronic acid, became familiar to enthusiasts.

Where the French buy it today

Korean beauty is available in France through major beauty retail chains, pharmacies, department stores, general online marketplaces and specialty online shops. Specific brand line-ups evolve over time, so shoppers often compare options. As with imported cosmetics generally, it is wise to check ingredient lists and batch or expiry information and to be aware of standard customs considerations when ordering from outside the EU.

Key facts
  • France has a strong native skincare and pharmacy-beauty tradition.
  • K-beauty offered novelty in textures, formats and ingredients.
  • Cushion compacts and sheet masks drew particular interest.
  • Hallyu broadened interest among younger French consumers.
  • K-beauty is sold via major retailers, pharmacies and online shops.
