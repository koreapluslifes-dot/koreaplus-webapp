The irritation loop: why strong actives often deepen dark marks on skin that pigments easily

⏱ 2 min read
⚡ Quick answer: On skin that pigments after inflammation, strong actives can create the very marks they were bought to fade, because irritation is itself a pigment trigger — a course of high-strength acids or a scrub can leave a darker patch than the original spot did. The way out is unglamorous: fewer actives, lower strengths, longer runs, and sun protection doing the heavy lifting. Korean routines happen to be well built for this.

The loop

Buy a strong exfoliant for dark marks. It irritates. Irritation is inflammation. Inflammation stimulates pigment. The new marks look like the old ones, so the instinctive response is to use the product more often, which produces more irritation. People stay in that loop for years, and it costs more the more melanin the skin has, because every round of inflammation cashes out as visible colour rather than as transient redness. The tell is a face that gets blotchier the harder you work at it, usually alongside tightness, stinging on application, or sudden intolerance of products that were previously fine.

Why the Korean approach suits this skin

The domestic Korean routine leans on low concentrations applied consistently, layered hydration, and an entire shelf of 진정 (jinjeong, calming) products built around centella, panthenol, mugwort and heartleaf. Whatever you make of the ten-step framing, that structure is close to ideal for skin that punishes irritation: the active is one small component of a routine designed not to provoke. Compare it with the habit of stacking a retinoid, a high-strength acid and a vitamin C on top of a foaming cleanser and expecting the barrier to keep up. One of those approaches is built for mark-prone skin.

Introducing an active without paying for it

One new product at a time, with a fortnight between additions, so you know what caused what. Start at the lowest available strength and at twice a week rather than nightly. Apply over a light moisturiser if it stings, which buffers without cancelling it. Stop at the first sign of persistent tightness or flaking rather than pushing through — pushing through is the advice that builds the loop. Patch-test along the jaw for several days rather than once. And do not start anything new during a period when you cannot be reliable about sunscreen.

The genuinely risky home ground

Some things deserve naming plainly: high-percentage peels bought online, dermarollers and other devices that break the surface, physical scrubs on inflamed skin, squeezing, and folk remedies involving bleach or hydrogen peroxide. On skin prone to marking, each can produce pigment change or scarring that outlasts whatever prompted it by years. Procedures on this skin are not automatically off the table, but they are a clinical decision made by someone who treats your skin type regularly — not a purchase. If you are considering one, that conversation starts with a dermatologist.
