쿠션 (kusyeon) compacts on deeper skin: shade depth, flashback and the oxidation myth

Why the range starts high

A cushion is liquid base held inside a saturated sponge and dispensed by pressing a puff against it. Korean ranges have historically offered 21호 and 23호 with a light, faintly pink lean that suits the domestic market. Some brands now go considerably further, but ladders still tend to bunch at the light end and thin out below the middle, with fewer undertone options exactly where you most need them. The format is not the problem. If a range does not reach you, no amount of pressing technique will make a two-step-too-light base look right.

Flashback, and how to test for it

Most cushions carry an SPF claim, and the filters delivering it frequently include titanium dioxide or zinc oxide. Those particles scatter visible light, which is unnoticeable in daylight at cushion levels but can bounce back as a pale mask under a camera flash. The effect is far more obvious on deeper skin, because the contrast against your own colour is greater. Test before you rely on it: apply as normal, then photograph yourself with the flash on in a dim room. If the face reads lighter than the neck, that product is daylight-only.

Settling is not oxidation

The complaint that cushions turn orange within an hour is usually describing something simpler. A freshly pressed layer is wet, thin and full of volatile ingredients; as those evaporate the film settles onto the skin and reads closer to its true depth, which is generally deeper than it looked going on. On deeper skin the shift is more visible, so the accusation gets made more often. Genuine oxidation exists and is over-blamed. Judge a match roughly twenty minutes after application, in daylight, at the jawline, and buy the deeper of two shades.

Technique and rescue

Refills make mixing cheap: buy two adjacent shades, or one cushion plus a pigment concentrate, and press from both. Build coverage with repeated thin presses rather than pressure, letting each pass set for a few seconds so the next one does not lift it. Warm the perimeter with a cream bronzer if the base sits slightly light. Set with a powder matched to your depth rather than a translucent one, since many translucent powders carry enough white pigment to undo the match. And treat the SPF figure as a bonus, never as your sun protection.
