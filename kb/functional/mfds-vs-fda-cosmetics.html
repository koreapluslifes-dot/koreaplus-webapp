MFDS vs FDA — why the same product is regulated so differently

⏱ 2 min read

The structural difference

Korea: general cosmetics, functional cosmetics, and pharmaceuticals. The United States: cosmetics and drugs, with anything claiming to affect structure or function landing in the drug category. That middle tier is the whole difference.

The sunscreen consequence

Because sunscreen is a drug in the US, adding a new UV filter requires a drug approval process that has proved extraordinarily slow. The logjam cracked in June 2026, when bemotrizinol (Tinosorb S) became the first new filter added to the US monograph since the 1990s — and it got there through the order pathway created in 2020, not the process that had stalled everything before it. As of writing most of the rest, Tinosorb M, Uvinul A Plus and Uvinul T 150 among them, are still unavailable to American formulators.

The claims consequence

A Korean product may state a reviewed brightening or wrinkle-improvement claim. An American cosmetic making the same claim would risk being treated as an unapproved drug, which is why US packaging leans on vaguer appearance language.

Neither system is simply better

Korea's approach moves faster and gives shoppers more information; the US approach demands more evidence before a claim can be made at all. The practical result for a K-beauty shopper is a market where sunscreen is genuinely more advanced and claims are more specific.
