Polyglutamic Acid (PGA) vs Birch Sap: which Korean skincare ingredient?

⏱ 1 min read
⚡ Quick answer: Polyglutamic Acid (PGA) vs Birch Sap: Polyglutamic Acid (PGA) leans toward dehydration (water-poor skin), while Birch Sap targets dehydration (water-poor skin). Can you use both? Yes — they layer well together.

Polyglutamic Acid (PGA)Birch Sap
What it isA fermentation-derived humectant that originates in natto, the Japanese fermented soybean. It is bigger than hyaluronic acid, so it behaves more like a moisture-holding film than a deep hydrator — which is why the two are often stacked.Round Lab built a hero line on it. Substituting a botanical sap for plain water is partly formulation, partly storytelling — the appeal is the light, clean feel rather than any single dramatic active.
Best forDehydration (water-poor skin), Dryness & dehydration, Rough & uneven textureDehydration (water-poor skin), Reactive & easily-stung skin, Dryness & dehydration

Which should you choose?

Choose Polyglutamic Acid (PGA) if your priority is Dehydration (water-poor skin); choose Birch Sap for Dehydration (water-poor skin). Many Korean routines use both — yes — they layer well together.
