Omija (Schisandra) vs Danggwi (Angelica gigas): which Korean skincare ingredient?

⏱ 1 min read
⚡ Quick answer: Omija (Schisandra) vs Danggwi (Angelica gigas): Omija (Schisandra) leans toward dullness & glow, while Danggwi (Angelica gigas) targets dullness & glow. Can you use both? Yes — they layer well together.

Omija (Schisandra)Danggwi (Angelica gigas)
What it is오미자 (omija) means five-flavour berry, and it has been a 한방 (hanbang) tonic ingredient for centuries. In cosmetics it is a competent botanical antioxidant with a good phenolic profile. Most of the research sits in test tubes rather than on faces, so treat it as supporting cast alongside a vitamin C or niacinamide.당귀 (danggwi) is the Korean angelica that appears in almost every hanbang blend, usually alongside peony and rehmannia. Topically it is an antioxidant botanical with reasonable laboratory backing and very little human skin data. It is also a herb with real internal pharmacology, so if you are pregnant, ask your doctor rather than assuming.
Best forDullness & glow, Sun damage & photoageing, Anti-ageing & firmnessDullness & glow, Anti-ageing & firmness, Redness & sensitivity
When to useBOTHBOTH

Which should you choose?

Choose Omija (Schisandra) if your priority is Dullness & glow; choose Danggwi (Angelica gigas) for Dullness & glow. Many Korean routines use both — yes — they layer well together.
