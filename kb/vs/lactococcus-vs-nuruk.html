Lactococcus Ferment Lysate vs Nuruk (Aspergillus) Ferment: which Korean skincare ingredient?

⏱ 1 min read
⚡ Quick answer: Lactococcus Ferment Lysate vs Nuruk (Aspergillus) Ferment: Lactococcus Ferment Lysate leans toward damaged moisture barrier, while Nuruk (Aspergillus) Ferment targets dullness & glow. Can you use both? Yes — they layer well together.

What it isOne of the bacterial ferments Korean brands use when they want to say microbiome without saying anything checkable. What reaches the bottle is a filtered soup of peptides, sugars and acids — nothing living. It behaves as a conditioning agent. Claims that it rebalances your skin's own microbial population are well beyond what has been shown.누룩 (nuruk) is the fermentation starter behind Korean rice wine, and the folklore about brewers' soft hands is the same story Japan tells about sake. Cosmetically it delivers what grain ferments deliver: amino acids, mild acids, sugars. Conditioning, pleasant, and considerably better documented as heritage than as an active.
Best forDamaged moisture barrier, Dullness & glow, Reactive & easily-stung skinDullness & glow, Rough & uneven texture

Which should you choose?

Choose Lactococcus Ferment Lysate if your priority is Damaged moisture barrier; choose Nuruk (Aspergillus) Ferment for Dullness & glow. Many Korean routines use both — yes — they layer well together.
