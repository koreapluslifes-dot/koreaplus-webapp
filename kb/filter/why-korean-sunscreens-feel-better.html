Why Korean sunscreens feel better than the ones you grew up with

⏱ 2 min read
⚡ Quick answer: It is mostly regulatory, not magical. Korea approves a range of modern organic UV filters — Uvinul A Plus, Tinosorb S and M, Uvinul T 150 among them — that give strong UVA protection in elegant, non-greasy textures. The filter list is the whole story

Sunscreen texture is decided largely by which UV filters a formulator is allowed to use. Korea, the EU, Japan and Australia permit a set of newer organic filters that are photostable, effective in the UVA range, and pleasant to formulate with. The United States regulates sunscreen as an over-the-counter drug, which has made adding new filters extraordinarily slow — as of writing most of the newer ones have still never been approved there, the single exception being bemotrizinol (Tinosorb S), cleared in June 2026 after a gap of nearly thirty years.

What that means on your face

Older filters often need higher concentrations and more supporting solvents to reach the same protection, which is what produces the thick, white, greasy feeling many people associate with sunscreen. Newer filters achieve strong protection at lower use levels and dissolve more readily, so the finished product can be a light fluid or a watery gel instead of a heavy cream.

Why this matters more than the SPF number

The most effective sunscreen is the one you will actually reapply. A pleasant texture is not a vanity concern — it is the difference between a routine you keep and one you abandon by July. That is the real reason Korean sunscreen became a global export category.

Reading a Korean sunscreen label

Look for the SPF number for UVB and the PA rating (PA+ through PA++++) for UVA, which is the system used across Korea and Japan. Then read the filter list itself. Recognising two or three modern filters tells you more about how a sunscreen will wear than any marketing claim on the front of the tube.

🔎 Regulatory note. UV-filter approval differs by market — a filter permitted in Korea, the EU or Japan may not be approved elsewhere. Always check the label of the product sold in your own country.
