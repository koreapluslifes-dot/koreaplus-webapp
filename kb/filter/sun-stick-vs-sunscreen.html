Korean sun sticks — convenient, but are you applying enough?

⏱ 1 min read
⚡ Quick answer: Sun sticks contain real, tested filters and they work, with one significant caveat: it is very difficult to apply the tested amount by swiping a stick. Treat them as a reapplication tool over makeup or on the go, not as your primary morning sunscreen.

Sun sticks contain real, tested filters and they work, with one significant caveat: it is very difficult to apply the tested amount by swiping a stick. Treat them as a reapplication tool over makeup or on the go, not as your primary morning sunscreen.

The format Korea popularised

A solid balm of sunscreen in a twist-up tube, designed to be swiped straight onto skin without touching it with your hands. It reapplies over makeup, travels well, and does not run — which is why it took over Korean handbags and then everyone else's.

The application-amount problem

SPF is tested at a specific amount per area of skin. Reaching that amount with a stick requires far more passes than most people make — typically many more than the two or three swipes that feel sufficient. Under-apply, and the protection you get is well below the label.

How to use one properly

Swipe repeatedly until skin looks visibly coated, then press it in with clean fingers. If that feels like too much product, it is roughly the right amount.

The honest recommendation

Apply a fluid or cream sunscreen in the morning where you can control the amount, and carry a stick for reapplication over the day. Used that way it is genuinely one of Korea's better format inventions.

🔎 Regulatory note. UV-filter approval differs by market — a filter permitted in Korea, the EU or Japan may not be approved elsewhere. Always check the label of the product sold in your own country.
