Water, velvet, glow and jelly: what Korean lip tint labels actually mean

⏱ 3 min read
⚡ Quick answer: The format words on Korean lip tints describe finish and vehicle rather than any standard: water tints are thin, fast-drying stains, velvet tints dry down soft-matte, and glow, jelly and water-gel tints stay shiny and mobile. As a rule of thumb, wear time rises as shine and comfort fall. None of the terms are regulated, so two brands can use velvet for quite different textures.

Water and stain types

A water tint is mostly dye and solvent with very little left behind to hold it in place — thin, almost runny, and dry within seconds. It stains rather than coats, which is why it survives a glass of water and why it is the traditional tool for a gradient lip. The trade-off is unforgiving application: on dry or flaking lips the dye grabs unevenly, and there is no emollient in the formula to soften the result afterwards. Exfoliate gently, blot balm off before applying, and build two light passes rather than one heavy one.

Velvet, matte and mousse

Velvet formulas suspend pigment in a fast-evaporating emulsion with film formers left behind on the lip. They apply creamy, set to a soft matte within a minute, and resist transfer well while feeling noticeably drier as the hours pass. Mousse and blur variants are the same idea with more powder for a diffused edge. That set film is what buys the wear time, so anything oily will break it — balm on top, a gloss, even a rich sunscreen underneath. If you want comfort back, add the balm once the day is over rather than halfway through it.

Glow, jelly and oil-based tints

Glow, jelly, water-gel and lip-oil tints keep pigment suspended in oils, esters or a gel network that never fully sets. They feel cushiony and look plump because the surface stays wet and reflective, and they flatter lip texture instead of drawing attention to it. They also transfer onto cups, migrate past the lip line on warm days, and want reapplying after anything you eat. A common compromise is a thin stain underneath for colour that survives, with a glow layer over the top for a finish you can renew as often as you like.

Choosing between them

Decide by what the lip has to survive rather than by the shade card. A long meal, a mask, no chance to touch up — velvet. A fresh look, frequent reapplication, dry lips — glow or jelly. A blurred gradient — a water tint. It is worth saying plainly that no formula is simultaneously maximum wear and maximum comfort: the same set film that stops transfer is the thing that feels tight by evening. Marketing that promises both is describing a compromise, and usually the compromise is that it wears off in patches instead of all at once.
