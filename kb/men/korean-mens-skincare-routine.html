A Korean skincare routine for men, without the ten steps

Start with three: a gentle low-pH cleanser, a light moisturiser and sunscreen every morning. Add a soothing product if shaving leaves skin reactive, and a treatment active only once those three are habit. Skin is skin — the actives do not change by gender, only the textures and marketing do.

Why Korea normalised it first

Korea has one of the world's highest rates of men's skincare use, and men's ranges sit on ordinary shelves rather than in a separate corner. That normality is the actual advantage — the products are unremarkable, so using them is too.

The three that matter

A gentle cleanser formulated near skin's own pH rather than a stripping foaming wash. A light moisturiser, because male skin being oilier on average does not mean it needs no moisture. And sunscreen, which does more than everything else combined.

What differs in practice

Male skin tends to be thicker, oilier and with larger pores on average, and shaving both irritates and mildly exfoliates. That combination argues for lighter textures, fragrance-free formulas, and going easy on any additional exfoliation.

What to add later

Once the base three are automatic, a niacinamide serum for oil and tone, or a retinoid at night for texture and lines. Adding actives before the basics are habit is how routines collapse.

How many steps should a Korean skincare routine have?

As few as three: cleanser, moisturiser and sunscreen. The ten-step routine was a Western marketing frame rather than a Korean rule — most Koreans use a handful of products daily and add extras occasionally.

What is the 10-step Korean skincare routine?

The 10-step Korean skincare routine is a layered framework popularized in Korea: oil cleanser, water cleanser, exfoliant, toner, essence, serum or ampoule, sheet mask, eye cream, moisturizer and sunscreen. It's a customizable menu, not a daily mandate, and most people use only the steps their skin needs.

Do you really need all 10 steps of Korean skincare?

No. The 10-step routine is a menu, not a requirement. Many Koreans use a simple core of cleanser, hydrating toner, moisturizer and sunscreen, adding serums, essences or masks only as their skin needs. The point is consistency and customization, not maximum steps.

What is a good Korean skincare routine for beginners?

A simple beginner Korean routine is a gentle cleanser, a hydrating toner, a lightweight moisturizer and daily sunscreen, adding one targeted serum once your skin adjusts. Introduce actives slowly, one at a time, and patch-test, following the Korean principle of gentle, barrier-friendly care over many steps at once.
