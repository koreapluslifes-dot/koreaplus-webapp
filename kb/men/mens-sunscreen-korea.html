Getting men to wear sunscreen: the texture argument

⏱ 1 min read
⚡ Quick answer: The obstacle is almost never belief, it is feel — greasy, white, sweat-stinging sunscreen does not get reapplied. Korean sunscreens solve exactly that, which is why they became the entry point into skincare for a lot of men.

The obstacle is almost never belief, it is feel — greasy, white, sweat-stinging sunscreen does not get reapplied. Korean sunscreens solve exactly that, which is why they became the entry point into skincare for a lot of men.

The objection is legitimate

Most people who avoid sunscreen are not disputing the evidence; they dislike how it feels. That is a solvable formulation problem rather than a character flaw, and it is worth naming honestly.

What to look for

A fluid or gel texture rather than a cream, alcohol in the formula for a faster dry-down if you are oily, and a matte or natural finish. Korean sunscreens offer all three because the modern filters available there make light textures possible.

The hairline and ears

The spots men miss most, and the spots dermatologists see most damage on — scalp part lines, the tops of ears, and the back of the neck. A stick or a spray handles the hairline more easily than a cream.

Reapplication without a bathroom trip

A sun stick in a bag or car is the practical answer. Just remember it needs many more passes than feel sufficient to reach a real amount.

Why is Korean sunscreen considered better?

Korean sunscreens are often praised for elegant, lightweight textures that feel pleasant enough to wear daily, which encourages consistent use. They frequently use newer filters approved in Korea but not the US. Preferences are partly cosmetic; effectiveness depends on applying enough and reapplying regardless of brand.

Why is Korean sunscreen better?

Because Korea approves modern UV filters — Uvinul A Plus, Tinosorb S and M among them — that give strong UVA protection in light, non-greasy textures. Some markets, notably the US, have not approved them.

Is Korean skincare different for men?

Skin is skin — the actives that work do not change by gender. What differs is context: shaving irritates and mildly exfoliates, male skin tends to be thicker and oilier on average, and men's ranges are usually formulated lighter and unscented.
