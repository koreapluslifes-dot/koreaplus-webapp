Does your skin really build tolerance to skincare?

⏱ 1 min read
⚡ Quick answer: Mostly no. Skin does not develop tolerance to skincare the way the body does to a drug. What people interpret as a product stopping work is usually the plateau after initial improvement, a seasonal change, or simply forgetting how their skin looked before. There is no need to rotate products to keep them effective.

Where the idea comes from

A new effective product produces a noticeable initial change. Once skin reaches its new baseline, further change slows — which feels like the product stopping. It has not; it is maintaining a result you have stopped noticing.

The genuine exception

Retinoid adjustment works the other way round: skin becomes more tolerant of the irritation over time, which is why you can often increase frequency or strength after a few months. That is building tolerance to a side effect, not losing the benefit.

What actually changes results

Season and humidity, hormonal shifts, a formula reformulation, a product past its shelf life, or a change elsewhere in your routine. Any of these is far more likely than skin becoming immune to niacinamide.

So should you rotate?

Not for tolerance reasons. Rotating for a genuine seasonal need — a lighter texture in summer, richer in winter — makes sense. Rotating to stop products losing effect is solving a problem that does not exist.

Do you really need all 10 steps of Korean skincare?

No. The 10-step routine is a menu, not a requirement. Many Koreans use a simple core of cleanser, hydrating toner, moisturizer and sunscreen, adding serums, essences or masks only as their skin needs. The point is consistency and customization, not maximum steps.

Do I really need to double cleanse?

At night, if you wear sunscreen or makeup — yes. Water-based cleansers cannot break down water-resistant sunscreen, so a single cleanse leaves a film behind. In the morning, one gentle cleanse or just water is plenty.
