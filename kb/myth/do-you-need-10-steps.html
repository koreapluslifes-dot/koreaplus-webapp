The 10-step Korean routine: marketing invention or real practice?

⏱ 1 min read
⚡ Quick answer: No. The ten-step routine was largely a Western marketing framing of Korean habits, not a rule Korean people follow. Most Koreans use a handful of products daily and add steps occasionally. A cleanser, a moisturiser and sunscreen is a complete routine; everything else is optional refinement.

No. The ten-step routine was largely a Western marketing framing of Korean habits, not a rule Korean people follow. Most Koreans use a handful of products daily and add steps occasionally. A cleanser, a moisturiser and sunscreen is a complete routine; everything else is optional refinement.

Where the ten steps came from

Korean routines genuinely tend to involve more, lighter layers than Western ones. That real habit was packaged for export as a numbered ten-step system — a marketing frame that made an unfamiliar approach legible, and sold a great deal of product in the process.

What Koreans actually do

The pattern is closer to a core daily routine of cleanse, hydrate, moisturise and protect, with occasional additions — a mask a few times a week, an exfoliant when needed, a treatment when something flares. Not ten products every night.

The genuine insight worth keeping

The valuable idea inside the marketing is layering thin hydration rather than relying on one heavy cream, and treating sunscreen as non-negotiable. Those two habits are worth adopting. The count is not.

How many steps you need

Cleanser, moisturiser, sunscreen. Add a treatment for a specific concern if you have one. Reactive skin does noticeably better on fewer products, not more.

What is the 10-step Korean skincare routine?

The 10-step Korean skincare routine is a layered framework popularized in Korea: oil cleanser, water cleanser, exfoliant, toner, essence, serum or ampoule, sheet mask, eye cream, moisturizer and sunscreen. It's a customizable menu, not a daily mandate, and most people use only the steps their skin needs.

Do you really need all 10 steps of Korean skincare?

No. The 10-step routine is a menu, not a requirement. Many Koreans use a simple core of cleanser, hydrating toner, moisturizer and sunscreen, adding serums, essences or masks only as their skin needs. The point is consistency and customization, not maximum steps.

Do I really need to double cleanse?

At night, if you wear sunscreen or makeup — yes. Water-based cleansers cannot break down water-resistant sunscreen, so a single cleanse leaves a film behind. In the morning, one gentle cleanse or just water is plenty.

What is a good Korean skincare routine for beginners?

A simple beginner Korean routine is a gentle cleanser, a hydrating toner, a lightweight moisturizer and daily sunscreen, adding one targeted serum once your skin adjusts. Introduce actives slowly, one at a time, and patch-test, following the Korean principle of gentle, barrier-friendly care over many steps at once.
