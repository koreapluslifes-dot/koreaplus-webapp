Will wearing sunscreen every day leave you short of vitamin D?

⏱ 1 min read
⚡ Quick answer: Real-world studies have generally not found that typical sunscreen use causes vitamin D deficiency — largely because people apply less than the tested amount and get incidental exposure anyway. Deliberately skipping sun protection is not a sensible way to manage vitamin D; diet and supplements are, and that is a conversation for a doctor.

Real-world studies have generally not found that typical sunscreen use causes vitamin D deficiency — largely because people apply less than the tested amount and get incidental exposure anyway. Deliberately skipping sun protection is not a sensible way to manage vitamin D; diet and supplements are, and that is a conversation for a doctor.

What the concern is based on

Sunscreen filters the UVB that skin uses to make vitamin D, so in principle perfect application would reduce production. The theory is sound; the practical effect is where it falls down.

Why it does not play out that way

Studies of everyday sunscreen users have generally not shown the deficiency the theory predicts. People apply thinly, miss areas, and spend unprotected time outdoors regardless. Incidental exposure adds up.

The risk trade-off

Vitamin D can be obtained from food and supplements. The cumulative effects of UV exposure — photoageing, pigmentation and skin cancer risk — cannot be undone as easily. Trading one for the other is a poor exchange.

If you are concerned

Ask your doctor about testing your levels and, if needed, supplementing. That is a medical question with a medical answer, not a reason to abandon sun protection.

Why is Korean sunscreen considered better?

Korean sunscreens are often praised for elegant, lightweight textures that feel pleasant enough to wear daily, which encourages consistent use. They frequently use newer filters approved in Korea but not the US. Preferences are partly cosmetic; effectiveness depends on applying enough and reapplying regardless of brand.

Can you use vitamin C and niacinamide together?

Yes, current understanding is that vitamin C and niacinamide can be used together safely, and many Korean formulas combine them. The old warning that they cancel out is largely outdated. If your skin is sensitive, you can still apply them at different times of day to be cautious.

Why is Korean sunscreen better?

Because Korea approves modern UV filters — Uvinul A Plus, Tinosorb S and M among them — that give strong UVA protection in light, non-greasy textures. Some markets, notably the US, have not approved them.

Can I use vitamin C and retinol together?

Not in the same layer, but easily in the same routine — vitamin C in the morning, retinol at night. That split suits both: vitamin C partners with sunscreen, and retinoids are degraded by light.
